What a share is worth — Edwin's EV model, decoded

Edwin sent a bundle on 9 August 2026. Inside it is the pricing model behind the front-end mock. This guide explains that model in plain English, checks its arithmetic, and answers the real question: how does it relate to the daily report we already receive?

✓ arithmetic re-derived and verified 6 items to put to Edwin source: InPlay-Handoff-George-FairValue-EV.md source: InPlay-Gamecast-Pricing-Spec.md (v3)

◎The 30-second version

Edwin's document answers one question: what is a share of a team worth at this instant? It does not cover order matching, spreads, or the market maker. It is the value number that everything else is priced around.

The model has two layers.

LAYER 1 · seasonFair(team)the resting price — what a share is worth when no game is live
▼  a game kicks off
LAYER 2 · lgValuePrice(…)the live price — Layer 1 re-priced play by play
▼  the crowd trades
traded price = pFair + εε is simulated noise in the mock — real order flow replaces it in production

The headline answer to your question is yes. The model is directly related to the daily report. Layer 1 is a stand-in for it. Edwin's mock runs in a browser with no feed, so it computes its own view of a team's remaining season from the IPO price and the results so far. In production that number does not get computed — it arrives every morning in Edwin's 06:00 ET file as expected_remaining_wins. Section 5 sets the two side by side.

⚠ Read this before you port anything

Layer 2 and our built model are not the same machine. They agree on the destination and disagree on the route. Both bank exactly $5 for a win. But the mock damps early-game moves, re-rates the rest of the season live, and marks a team down on an injury. Our engine does none of those three things. Section 6 is the full comparison.

01The one idea the whole model rests on

Price tracks value, not win probability. Win probability is an input to one component. It is never the price.

A share is a claim on a team-company's season revenue. That revenue has two sources, and both are fixed amounts:

  • On-field — a win pays $5.00 per share. Absolute. It never floats on the quality of the opponent.
  • Off-field — a pool of $2.50 per game, split between the two teams. A team's share of that pool is weighted by its brand.

So the value of a share is: money already banked, plus the money the rest of the season is expected to produce. Nothing else.

This is why the price can fall while a team is winning. The win adds $5. But if the team wins badly, or loses its quarterback in the process, the rest of the season is now worth less. If the second effect is bigger than $5, the share drops. A model built on win probability alone can never produce that. This is the whole point of the design.

02Layer 1 — the resting price

Between games, a share is worth its IPO price, corrected for everything we have learned since the IPO.

fair = IPO price + (the revenue we now expect this season) − (the revenue the IPO price already assumed)

If the season is going better than the IPO assumed, the share is worth more than the IPO price. If it is going worse, less. That is the entire idea.

How the model reads the IPO price

The model has no separate table of team strength. It derives strength from the IPO price. The NFL IPO range runs from $35.66 (Arizona) to $81.20 (LA Rams). A team's position in that range is called pct, and it drives three things: expected wins, off-field share, and star-player value.

// where a team sits in the league, 0 = cheapest, 1 = most expensive pct = clamp( (px − 35.66) / (81.20 − 35.66), 0, 1 ) // the three things pct drives e0 = 5.5 + pct * 6.5 // preseason expected wins → 5.5 to 12.0 offShare = 1.0 + pct * 0.5 // off-field $ per game → 1.00 to 1.50 starValue = 0.6 + 1.2 * pct // star-player multiplier → 0.6 to 1.8

The formula

wins, played, banked // results so far this season remGames = 17 − played pRem = clamp( (e0 + 2*wins) / (17 + 2*played), 0.08, 0.85 ) expRemWins = remGames * pRem expRemRev = remGames * (pRem * 5.25 + offShare) pre = e0 * 5.25 + 17 * offShare fair = clamp( px + (banked + expRemRev) − pre , 14, 118 )

Two lines deserve a note.

pRem — the forward win rate. It blends the prior with the form. e0 is what the IPO expected over 17 games. 2*wins over 2*played is the season so far, given double weight. Early in the season the prior dominates. Late in the season the form dominates. The clamp stops any team from being priced at certainty.

The two win coefficients. A banked win is exactly $5.00. A forecast win is worth $5.25. Edwin says to keep both and calls the extra 25 cents "a small blend". He does not say what it blends. See the asks in Section 7.

03Layer 2 — the live price

At kickoff the resting price becomes the anchor. Every play then adjusts it.

anchor = clamp( seasonFair(team).fair, 24, 96 ) pFair = anchor + banked + C1 + (C2 − C2₀) + (C4 − C4₀) + offCur
TermWhat it isHow fast it moves
anchorthe resting price from Layer 1fixed for the game
bankedwins banked earlier in the same session only. Zero for a single live game. Do not add the season's banked money here — it is already inside the anchor.step changes
C1this game's win premiumevery play
C2 − C2₀the re-rate of the remaining gamesslow, except on injury or a decisive scoreline
C4 − C4₀the re-rate of remaining off-field valueinjury only
offCurthis game's off-field moneylocked at kickoff

C1 — the win premium, and the time-decay dial

M = 0.35 + 0.65 * clamp(1 − timeLeftSecs/3600, 0, 1)^1.5 cgWin = 5 * ( wpPre + M * (wpLive − wpPre) ) C1 = cgWin − 5 * 0.5

M is the leverage dial. It controls how much a swing in live win probability is allowed to move the price yet. Early in a game the model is deliberately sceptical of the scoreboard. Late in a game it is not.

MomentMPrice move per 0.01 of win probability
Kickoff0.35$0.018
Halftime0.58$0.029
Two-minute warning0.97$0.048

A late tie game gets both a high M and a live win probability that swings violently on every play. The two multiply. That is why the last two minutes move the price hard and a Q1 first down barely registers.

This is the only place win probability enters the price.

C2 — where re-rating lives

This is the component that makes the model interesting, and it is the one our engine does not have.

domActual = clamp( (homeS − oppS) / 21, −1, 1 ) // what the scoreboard says domExpected = (wpPre − 0.5) * 1.5 // what a team this good SHOULD show perf = clamp( domActual − domExpected, −1, 1 ) reRate = clamp( 1 + 0.15 * rhoJit * perf, 0.80, 1.25 ) C2₀ = gRemAfter * winRateBase * 5 // remaining value at kickoff C2 = gRemAfter * (winRateBase * reRate * injOn) * 5

In words: the model compares how much a team is dominating against how much it should be dominating, given the pregame line. The gap re-rates the whole rest of the season, up to ±25%.

gRemAfter is the lever that decides how much this matters. With 9 games left, a re-rate moves a lot of money. With 1 game left, almost none. An unconvincing win in week 2 hurts far more than the same performance in week 16.

Injuries

The mock invents injuries at random. Production would take them from a real feed. Either way the impact formula is what ports:

hit = posWeight * starValue * severityFrac injOn = clamp( injOn * (1 − hit * 0.60 * rnd(0.7,1.3)), 0.40, 1 ) // hits C2 injOff = clamp( injOff * (1 − hit * 0.30 * rnd(0.7,1.3)), 0.55, 1 ) // hits C4
  • Position weights: QB 0.35 ≫ WR / DE / CB 0.09 ≫ LT / OL 0.06 ≫ RB / S 0.05 ≫ LB / TE 0.03.
  • Severity: 65% minor (0–0.05) · 25% moderate (0.10–0.30) · 10% major (0.50–1.00).
  • Star value comes from the IPO price, via pct. No hand-written table. A top team's quarterback marks the team down roughly twice as hard as a bottom team's.
  • Two-sided. Your star goes down: you are marked down, and the opponent's win probability rises. Their star goes down: the reverse.
  • An elite quarterback lost for the season can cut remaining on-field value by 40–60% in one play. This is the "Aaron Rodgers 2023" case Edwin names in the v3 spec.

Off-field

C4₀ = gRemAfter * offShare C4 = C4₀ * injOff offCur = offShare

Off-field value is a projection only. It never keys off live trading volume. Edwin is explicit about why: volume feeding price makes the price circular. Volume is disclosed on the weekly Friday report and never per team, live.

Your unit test

pFair = seasonVal + cgWin + offCur. Edwin's worked case: 65.93 + 3.10 + 1.42 = 70.45. If those three do not sum to pFair, something is wrong.

04The three behaviours the model exists to produce

Everything above is machinery. These three outcomes are the point of it.

  1. An upset is worth more than $5. A 30% underdog wins. The win banks $5, of which $3.50 was unpriced. perf goes strongly positive, the forward win rate is revised up, and C2 adds another ~$3.20. Total move ≈ +$6.70 on a $5 win.
  2. A favourite squeaking past a weak team can lose money. An 80% favourite wins by 2. Only $1.00 of the win was unpriced. But the team was expected to dominate and did not, so C2 is marked down ~$2.00. Total ≈ −$1.00 — the share falls on a win.
  3. A star injury drops a winning team. Team leads 14–3 in Q3. The quarterback is carted off. injOn falls to ~0.72, C2 loses ~$6.50. Net ≈ −$7 while winning.
✓ Tested in the mock

Edwin reports that a major injury to a leading team dropped its share price in 26 of 26 test cases (100%). That is the behaviour a win-probability price can never produce, and it is the strongest evidence the design does what it claims.

05The daily report — the connection you were looking for

Yes. They are the same quantity, produced two different ways.

Every morning at 06:00 ET we receive one JSON file from Edwin. It carries all 170 teams — 32 NFL and 138 college — and it is published even when nothing has changed. Per team it carries:

{ "team_id": "sr:competitor:nfl0", "league": "NFL", "effective_time": "2026-08-29T10:00:00Z", "revision": 1, "is_correction": false, "expected_remaining_wins": 11.60137430577575, "sigma": 1.9035016198976913, "games_remaining": 17, "methodology_version": "1.0.0" }

The bolded field is T. It is the single most important number in our pricing engine. Our on-field value is built directly from it:

on-field = $5 × ( T − Σ p_ref(g) + Σ x_g ) over g ∈ G

Without T, a security has no price at all — our code refuses to construct one.

So where does Edwin's mock get T?

It does not have one. The mock runs entirely in a browser with no feed. So it manufactures its own equivalent from the only two things it has: the IPO price and the results so far.

Production — what we built

T arrives. Edwin's engine computes it: MOV-capped Elo for college, raked to the de-vigged sportsbook total for the NFL. We read it and use it.

  • Source: the 06:00 ET file
  • Covers all 170 teams
  • Rebuilt every morning
  • Carries sigma and a methodology version
  • Reflects the betting market's view

The mock — what Edwin sent

T is invented. expRemWins = remGames × pRem, where pRem blends the IPO-implied win total with the season record so far.

  • Source: the IPO price plus results
  • Covers 32 NFL teams only
  • Recomputed on demand, in the browser
  • No uncertainty measure
  • Reflects nothing outside the mock
Layer 1 of Edwin's document is the placeholder for the daily report. It is what the mock does because it cannot receive the file. Do not port it — we already have the real thing, and the real thing is better.

Why the mock's version cannot be used in production anyway

  • It is NFL-only by construction. Every input keys off pct, which is a position within the NFL IPO range of $35.66–$81.20. The 138 college teams have no place in it. We already established that no Sportradar product carries college season win totals, which is exactly why Edwin's own engine exists.
  • It hard-codes a 17-game season (remGames = 17 − played). The daily file carries games_remaining per team, which is the correct source.
  • It has no uncertainty measure. The daily file carries sigma. Our reference-price work already needed it.
✓ Where the two models genuinely agree

Both price a win at a flat $5.00. Both treat off-field as a $2.50-per-game pool split by brand. Both freeze the pregame win probability at kickoff and price the live game as the movement away from it. Both insist the traded print is not the fair value. That is a lot of agreement, and it is the part that matters.

06Where the mock and our engine actually differ

This is the section to read before anyone ports anything. Both models bank exactly $5 for a win, and both land in the same place at the final whistle. The route between kickoff and whistle is different in four ways.

QuestionEdwin's mockWhat we built
Expected remaining wins computed in-browser from IPO price + form T, read from the 06:00 ET daily file
Value of a win $5.00 flat $5.00 flat  ·  tie $2.50
Live game effect four components: C1, ΔC2, ΔC4, offCur one term: $5 × (x_g − p_ref(g))
Time decay on early moves yes — M runs 0.35 → 0.97 none — a Q1 move hits at full $5 weight
Live re-rate of the rest of the season yes — reRate, ±25% none — it only moves when tomorrow's file lands
Injury markdown explicit — injOn / injOff no direct path; reaches price only via live WP, then via tomorrow's T
Off-field 1.0 + pct × 0.5 per game, injury-sensitive RAV + EAV — mocked, static construction inputs; methodology is Edwin's and unbuilt
Pregame probability wpPre, set at kickoff p_ref(g), frozen at kickoff (N22)
After the whistle the adjustment persists G is a set; a game leaves only when a new T absorbs it
Traded price pFair + simulated ε real order flow; the MM quotes a fixed spread around RP

The difference that will show up first

The time-decay dial. Take a Q1 play that moves win probability by 0.03.

ModelPrice moveSensitivity at kickoff
Edwin's mock$0.05$5 × 0.35 = $1.75 per unit of probability
Our engine$0.15$5.00 per unit of probability

Our price is roughly 2.9× more sensitive in the first quarter, and it stays more sensitive until late in the game, when the two converge. Both still bank exactly $5 at the whistle. Only the path differs. If the app shows a Gamecast price beside the real traded price, the two will visibly disagree in the first half, and that is a product problem before it is an engineering one.

⚠ The design question underneath all of this

Our engine cannot re-rate the rest of the season during a game. That is not an omission — it is the direct consequence of taking T from a daily file. The rest of the season is Edwin's number, and it changes once a day. So the three headline behaviours in Section 4 do not exist in our price today. An injury does not mark us down. An unconvincing win does not mark us down. Those effects only appear the next morning, when a new T lands.

Adding a live re-rate on top of T would double-count the following morning, because Edwin's engine rebuilds T from the same evidence. This needs his ruling, not our assumption.

07What to put to Edwin

Six items. The first is the one that matters.

  1. Does the live re-rate belong in production at all? The mock re-rates the rest of the season play by play. Our engine takes that number from your daily file. If we add a live re-rate on top of T, tomorrow's file will re-price the same evidence a second time. Which is intended — a static T with all movement in the current game, or a live re-rate that tomorrow's T must then be built to avoid repeating?
  2. Is the time-decay dial M meant to be in the real price? Our on-field leg has no equivalent, so we are ~2.9× more sensitive to a Q1 probability move than the Gamecast is. One of the two is wrong, or the Gamecast is a display model and the traded price is not meant to match it. We need to know which.
  3. The KC worked example points the wrong way. §1.4 says the share is up from its IPO. Back-solving your own formulas gives an IPO price of ≈$73.94 against a fair value of $68.93 — it is down $5.01. The model is behaving correctly; the sentence is wrong. Confirm, so the example can be used for teaching.
  4. The offShare range is stated two ways. §1.2's comment says ≈1.0–1.9, but the formula 1.0 + pct × 0.5 yields 1.0–1.5. The v3 spec §4 says 1.0–1.9 is the dial target and 1.0–1.5 is what is coded. Confirm which one production should use.
  5. Star values disagree between your two documents. The EV doc gives KC ≈1.61 and CAR ≈1.10. The v3 spec Appendix A gives KC 1.57 and CAR 0.83. The formula is the same, so the difference is the pct basis — IPO price percentile in one, seasonFairPct in the other. Which basis is correct?
  6. What is the extra 25 cents in $5.25? The doc says to use $5.00 for a banked win and $5.25 for a forecast win, and calls the difference "a small blend". Our engine uses $5.00 throughout. We cannot adopt a coefficient without knowing what it represents.
