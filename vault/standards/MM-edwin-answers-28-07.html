Item 1Method — yes, but don't use the posted line raw.

Plain English: the posted total is the median — where the money splits — with the bookmaker's margin baked into the prices on each side. The odds on Over vs Under tell us which side of the line the true average sits on, and by how much. So: turn both sides' odds into probabilities, strip the margin, and shift the line toward whichever side the fair probability favors, scaled by σmkt.

Four steps:

  1. convert the American odds on Over and Under to implied probabilities
  2. de-vig — divide each by their sum so they add to exactly 1
  3. take σmkt for the league (2.7 NFL, 2.2 NCAA)
  4. mean = line + σmkt × inverse_normal_cdf(fair over probability)
mean  =  line  +  σmkt × InvNormCDF( Pover fair )
the only place σmkt is ever used — 2.7 NFL, 2.2 NCAA

Item 2The double count — your fix is right.

The convention, using the definitions above:

On-field value per share  =  $5 × ( T  −  Σg∈G pref(g)  +  Σg∈G xg )
subtract what T assumed for each kicked-off game, add what it is actually doing

Scope note So this formula isn't misread as the whole price: this is the ON-FIELD LEG of the security. Per the build spec, the full share value is the retained-earnings composite — known Retained Earnings Balance already banked, plus this on-field leg, plus expected ad-revenue accrual from the $2.50-per-game commercial pool. The ad leg stacks on top of this formula exactly as the build spec already describes; nothing here changes it.

Implementation

  • Unit test (a): the on-field leg equals $5 × T at the first kickoff after a fresh T.
  • Unit test (b): during a game, it moves $5 per unit of live probability — nothing frozen.
  • Unit test (c): after the final whistle, it holds at banked-plus-expected until the next T arrives.
  • Build G as a set and the adjustment as a sum from day one — on college Saturdays, or any week the total lags, you'll be netting out two or more games.
  • When a new T lands, do not smooth the mid. Widen quotes around publication windows if you want cover.
The price change when a new T arrives is a discontinuous repricing reflecting newly available information, not market-maker behavior. Smoothing it would mean quoting a price you know is wrong.

All three tests — plus one proving your $63 example cannot happen — are in the suite, against inplay_feed/pricing.py. TeamPricer in that module is a reference implementation of the full on-field lifecycle; lift it verbatim if you want.

Item 3College — the number comes from the attached engine.

Complete This section is the complete spec; nothing further is coming for this item except S3 credentials.

How it's computed — all in the zip, module by module

  • Every team carries a power rating, updated after each result — think chess Elo (inplay_feed/elo.py). Margin of victory counts but is capped, so a blowout of a weak opponent doesn't distort. Home field is a fixed bump. Preseason ratings start from last season, pulled back toward the conference average.
  • A rating gap converts to a win probability through a logistic curve, calibrated weekly against Sportradar's own posted pregame probabilities where both exist (inplay_feed/calibration.py) — so the published numbers and your live feed cannot drift apart systematically.
  • NFL only: remaining-game probabilities are rescaled so they sum exactly to the de-vigged sportsbook total from item 1 (inplay_feed/rake.py). For the NFL, the published number IS the sportsbook number — ratings only supply the game-by-game shape.
  • Expected remaining wins = the sum of those probabilities over the remaining schedule; the feed's σ field is the schedule dispersion off the same probabilities.

Seeding

Seeding the ratings needs only one to two seasons of final scores — nflverse for NFL, the collegefootballdata.com API for college, both free, no odds needed — loaded into the row format in backtest.py ({season, home, away, home_score, away_score, neutral}), replayed chronologically through EloEngine, then preseason_reset. Ship on the default parameters in the code (K 24, HFA 55, MOV cap 28 — standard football-Elo values); the grid search is confirmation, not a blocker.

Consumption rules — deliberately identical to item 4

  • hold last value until a new record arrives
  • on is_correction = true, replace the record with the same effective_time and lower revision

Item 4Spec of the number.

Remaining games only — banked wins never appear in it. (Deliberate contrast with T, which is whole-season; the definitions block governs.) Daily 06:00 ET, heartbeat even when unchanged, hold last value. Corrections: reissued with the original effective timestamp, a bumped revision, and the is_correction flag — never fixed silently in the next publish. The publisher module enforces all of this, including refusing to write a file that fails validation.

Item 5Ties.

Settlement rule first, pricing follows: SDMM-1 settles a tie as xg = 0.5 — $2.50 a share against the $5.00 win — mirroring NFL standings and matching the IPO tie leg. Price the two-way market exactly as you proposed. NFL ties run about 0.4% of games; the expected drag is on the order of half a cent a share — carry it as a small reserve, don't model it. College can't tie.

Item 6IPO prices — the complete methodology.

With the computation attached twice over: engine.py (the original) and inplay_feed/ipo.py in the zip (the same methodology as an importable module with its own tests).

The listing price of every one of the 170 Team Company securities follows one principle: expected terminal liquidating distribution per share, less a small rule-based primary-market concession. No discretionary input anywhere.
IPO price  =  EV  −  discount

On-field leg

E[Wins] from single-book BetMGM season win-total prices at the freeze, de-vigged proportionally, mapped through μ = line + σmkt × InvNorm(Pover) — the exact item 1 math with the exact same constants, so your in-season T and the IPO on-field leg are the same computation by construction.

Off-field leg

No realized volume exists before trading opens, so expected capture comes from a deterministic Popularity Index:

Popularity Index  =  0.6 × Brand  +  0.4 × PerfIndex
Brand: a fixed hand-assigned 0–100 tier, supplied with the workbook
PerfIndex  =  100 × E[Wins]Games
Capture(i, j)  =  Clamp( PopiPopi + Popj,  0.20,  0.80 )
per-game capture against opponent j — Bradley-Terry

Opponents outside the 170-team universe are not contested: the universe team accrues the full $2.50.

Discount

1% to 3%, scaled within each league by the CONTESTED share of the name's off-field value. On-field value is already market-priced and carries no concession. Names whose uncontested accrual exceeds 20% of EV carry no discount and list at full EV. engine.py is authoritative on the exact scaling map; ipo.py flags the one line to reconcile against it before the freeze.

Mechanics for the build

Prices are computed once from the odds snapshot three days before the offering opens, at full precision — no rounding anywhere — and never revised: not for injuries, not for line moves, not for anything between freeze and close.

Day one The Reference Price seeds at IPO EV, not at the listed price — the discount is a primary-market concession, not a valuation statement — so your day-one mid starts at EV. Acceptance test for day-one agreement: run the engine on the freeze snapshot and match its output; there is nothing to reverse-engineer.

Float: a fixed, identical share count per team; the count follows separately — nothing on your side waits for it.
