# InPlay Advertising: ICP Definitions

> **Status:** draft v1, authored 12 August 2026 in working session with Edwin Johnson and Cody Haugen.
> **Structure:** ICP authoring structure, Cold Outreach Workforce (snapshot synced from the cofounder vault 2026-08-11).
> **Grounded on:** `inplay-advertising-offer.md`, same folder. Built-for line, eight pains, nine outcomes, two component tracks.
> **What this is:** the Tier 1, 2 and 3 weights below **are** the rubric the workforce grades every lead against. Tier 1 Business Type 50 per cent, Tier 2 Business Model 30 per cent, Tier 3 Firmographics 20 per cent. An A grade is 70 per cent or above. The ICP is authored in the shape it is scored.

## Segmentation

**Axis: where the money physically sits, and what it is being spent against.**

Vertical was considered and rejected. Edwin and Cody confirmed in session that the pitch does not change by industry: they open on what the audience does, how they trade and what they can trade, then show where units can be placed. Same pitch every time means vertical is not the segment.

Company size was rejected for the same reason. The $50,000 sponsorship minimum compresses the range, and two similarly sized companies buy this for entirely different reasons.

What genuinely separates buyers is where the decision sits. At an agency it sits with an investment lead placing other people's money and needing something no rival agency has. At a brand with in-house media it sits with a media buyer who owns the plan personally and is priced out of the surfaces that would distinguish it. At a locked-out brand it sits with a chief executive whose customer is this audience and who has no route to reach them.

| ICP | One-line identity | Dominant emotional pain |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · The Global Agency Investment Desk** | Holding-company or independent media agency placing US brand budgets | **SAMENESS** · paid to find what nobody else has, handed the same thing in new packaging |
| **2 · The Priced-Out Brand, buying direct** | Mid-market US consumer brand with an in-house media function and a fall campaign | **PRICED OUT** · the one place their customer is most reachable starts at six figures for an afternoon |
| **3 · The Locked-Out Category Brand** | Finance, fintech, crypto, content and alternative-category brands, chief-executive led | **NO ROUTE IN** · their exact customer assembles somewhere they are structurally unable to buy |

The pains are non-interchangeable. Group 1 lacks novelty to sell. Group 2 lacks money to buy. Group 3 lacks a door. The buying group shifts cleanly along the same axis: investment lead, in-house media buyer, chief executive.

**Evidence status, stated honestly.** Only ICP 1 has real conversation evidence behind it: Omnicom and WPP at global level, both engaged and both paused pending audience proof, at $150K for a point of view and $250K to $500K for a full campaign. ICP 2 has none, and no US brand-direct conversation has yet got far. ICP 3 rests on a single thread of financial-institution conversations. ICPs 2 and 3 are hypotheses derived from the offer, to be corrected as real conversations happen. The workforce should weight ICP 1 first.

## ICP 1 · The Global Agency Investment Desk

**1. ICP Archetype Name:** `Media Agency - The Differentiation-Starved Investment Desk`

**2. Core Thesis:** A media planning and buying organisation, holding company or independent, whose business is won and kept on its ability to bring clients reach and ideas the next agency cannot. Their plans are built from the same inventory every rival buys: the same networks, the same upfronts, the same exchanges. They live on pitch cycles and renewals, both decided largely on differentiation, and the one thing structurally hardest for them to manufacture is genuinely new, ownable inventory. They have the client budgets and the buying machinery already. Omnicom and WPP have told InPlay exactly this in conversation: they want new businesses, they liked the mechanism and the audience composition, and the only thing holding them is proof the audience exists. Their dominant emotional pain is **SAMENESS: they are paid to find what nobody else has, and the market keeps handing them the same thing in new packaging.**

**3. Rubric-Aligned Profile**

- **Tier 1: Specific Business Type (50% Weight)**
    - **Primary Industry:** Advertising and media agencies: planning, buying, investment, sponsorship.
    - **Ideal Verticals:** Holding-company media agencies (Omnicom Media Group, WPP and GroupM, Publicis Media, IPG Mediabrands, Dentsu); independent media agencies (Horizon, Tinuiti, Wpromote, Media.Monks); sports and entertainment marketing agencies (Wasserman, Octagon, rEvolution); performance and growth agencies with US brand rosters; experiential and brand-partnership shops.
    - **Defining Characteristics & Offerings (The "Idiot's Guide"):**
        - **Core Identity:** An organisation that plans and buys media or sponsorship for brand clients, paid on fees, commission or trading margin, whose competitive edge is the novelty and quality of what it can put on a plan.
        - **Website Evidence:** Client logo walls and case studies; service pages naming media planning, buying, investment or connections planning; an innovation, emerging-media or partnerships practice; new-business and awards language.
    - **State Modifiers (Observable Signals of this Archetype):**
        - **GTM Motion:** Hiring Investment Director, Digital Investment Director, Head of Innovation, Emerging Media Lead, Sports Investment Lead, Video Investment Director or Programmatic Director. New client-win announcements.
        - **Marketing Motion:** Thought leadership on new channels and attention; award-show entries; conference presence at Cannes, Possible and Advertising Week, where being first to a property is currency.
        - **Growth Signal:** Recent account wins or pitches in flight; expansion of an innovation or partnerships practice; press about principal-based media under client scrutiny.
- **Tier 2: Business Model Category (30% Weight)**
    - **Primary Model:** Services. Fee, commission or trading margin. Pitch and retain.
    - **Target Customer:** US brands with fall sports or second-half campaigns.
    - **Tech Stack Signal:** DV360, Campaign Manager 360, The Trade Desk or Xandr visible in job posts; SponsorUnited for sponsorship intelligence; IAS or DoubleVerify for verification. A visible DSP stack is the tell that they can transact programmatically without an insertion order.
- **Tier 3: Firmographics (20% Weight)**
    - **Employee Count (Range):** `50-50,000`
    - **Geography (Ideal):** `US offices with US brand clients`

**4. Disqualification Criteria (Anti-Signals)**

- **Creative-only or PR-only shops:** No media planning or buying function means no plan for a position to live on. Wrong organisation entirely.
- **In-house brand teams:** These are ICP 2 and ICP 3, not the agency channel. Do not double-count them here.
- **No client capable of a $150,000 point of view:** If no account on the roster could fund the entry conversation, the agency can admire the property but cannot transact it.
- **No US clients or no US audience:** The inventory is US only, eligible jurisdictions only. A roster with no US brands has nothing to spend here.
- **No programmatic buying capability:** Without a DSP they cannot use the path that removes the insertion-order objection, which is half the reason this ICP fits.
- **Agencies whose entire book is declined categories:** A shop servicing only betting, daily fantasy, sportsbook or casino clients has no spendable roster.

**5. Archetype-to-Pain Mapping (Sharpened for SAMENESS)**

Every signal below is evidence of an organisation whose value rests on differentiation it cannot easily source.

| Observable Signal (from Tiers 1 & 2) | Primary Inferred Pain (The Core Problem) | Relevance to the Offer (Why We Win) |
|---|---|---|
| An innovation or emerging-media practice page, or an Emerging Media Lead on the careers page | **`I am judged on bringing clients something new, and the market keeps handing me the same inventory.`** The practice exists to find the next thing, and most next things resolve to repackaged programmatic. The pressure to be first is constant and public. The supply of genuinely first things is not. Every year the differentiation they sourced last year becomes table stakes. | InPlay is an environment nobody has advertised in, because it did not exist. Field naming rights and a position inside a live trading session are not available anywhere else at any price a client could clear. The whole thing can be demonstrated on a phone in ninety seconds. |
| Heavy new-business and pitch activity, awards entries, "Agency of the Year" language | **`I win and lose accounts on differentiation I cannot manufacture.`** Pitches are decided on the idea the client has not heard before. When every agency proposes the same channels, price becomes the only lever and margin dies. Adopting a dud costs the agency's credibility, so the sorting job is most of their week. | A property no rival agency has seen restores a non-price reason to win. The offer's honest position, that InPlay is pre-launch and carries the delivery risk itself, is defensible in a pitch room in a way a forecast never is. |
| A client roster containing brands chasing young sports audiences: beverage, apparel, fintech, consumer tech | **`My clients keep asking for reach I cannot get them efficiently.`** Their clients want the football audience, and the agency is stuck offering broadcast they cannot afford or programmatic that does not convert. The gap between what the client asks for and what the agency can source is where accounts are lost. | One agency relationship places positions across several accounts. The $50,000 sponsorship minimum means several clients can enter rather than only the largest, so the property scales across the roster instead of being a one-off. |
| A visible DSP stack: DV360, The Trade Desk, Campaign Manager 360 | **`Anything needing a bespoke insertion order dies in my finance team.`** The agency transacts in its own platform and treats a custom order from an unfamiliar publisher as a compliance and finance problem, however good the idea. Deals stall in trafficking rather than in strategy. | InPlay accepts programmatic demand through the buyer's own DSP. The idea and the paperwork stop being the same decision, which removes the most common operational reason a good property never runs. |
| A sports and entertainment practice, or a Sports Investment Lead in the org | **`Every sports property I can offer is either unaffordable or already taken.`** Stadium, field and league inventory is held by national brands on multi-year deals, so the sports practice spends its time selling what is left over. Being the agency that could not get a client into football is a recurring, quiet failure. | Field naming rights inside InPlay are the stadium a client could never buy, at a price that clears, for a whole 142-day season, and they render in live play and in every simulation of a game. |

## ICP 2 · The Priced-Out Brand, buying direct

**1. ICP Archetype Name:** `Mid-Market US Consumer Brand - The Priced-Out Fall Buyer`

**2. Core Thesis:** A US consumer brand, roughly $30M to $500M in revenue, that runs its own media buying, has a named fall football campaign, and buys digital video and in-app inventory directly rather than through an agency of record. The person who owns that budget can approve a mid five-figure commitment inside a month without procurement, which is the single characteristic that makes this ICP buyable at $50,000. They are not priced out of media in general. They are priced out of the specific surfaces that would make their fall plan distinguishable, so the plan is assembled from the same connected TV, sports audio and social lines every competitor is buying. They have usually taken one swing at a novel sports property already, and it disappointed, which makes their scepticism specific rather than general. Their dominant emotional pain is **BEING PRICED OUT: the one place their customer is most reachable starts at a hundred thousand dollars for a single afternoon.**

**3. Rubric-Aligned Profile**

- **Tier 1: Specific Business Type (50% Weight)**
    - **Primary Industry:** US consumer brands and consumer services with an in-house media function.
    - **Ideal Verticals:** Energy, functional and ready-to-drink beverages; DTC and retail snacks and nutrition; apparel, footwear and performance wear; grooming and personal care; challenger mobile network operators; game-day food, delivery and challenger quick-service restaurants; consumer wearables and recovery.
    - **Defining Characteristics & Offerings (The "Idiot's Guide"):**
        - **Core Identity:** A branded consumer product or service whose buyer plausibly follows American football, where marketing rather than distribution drives growth, and where media is planned and bought by named employees rather than an agency of record. A research agent must confirm all three.
        - **Website Evidence:** A direct store or store locator; a partnerships, ambassador or athlete page; seasonal game-day or tailgate campaign content; promo codes in the footer indicating direct media buying; a careers page listing media, paid, growth or partnerships roles.
    - **State Modifiers (Observable Signals of this Archetype):**
        - **GTM Motion:** Hiring Media Manager, Head of Media, Paid Media Lead, Growth Marketing Manager, Sports Marketing Manager or Brand Partnerships. An open in-house media role is the single strongest signal that the money is not sitting at an agency.
        - **Marketing Motion:** Present in sports podcast host reads, detectable through unique promo codes; connected TV or streaming sports campaigns referenced in trade press; seasonal football creative published on owned social in August and September; creator or ambassador activity tied to the season.
        - **Growth Signal:** A funding round or retail expansion announced in the last 18 months; a new line or flavour timed to football season; a stated US second-half push; press naming a fall or game-day campaign.
- **Tier 2: Business Model Category (30% Weight)**
    - **Primary Model:** Direct to consumer and omnichannel retail. Marketing-led, in-house media operation.
    - **Target Customer:** US consumer, broadly 18 to 55, who follows American football.
    - **Tech Stack Signal:** Shopify or Shopify Plus; Klaviyo or Attentive for lifecycle; Triple Whale, Northbeam or Rockerbox for attribution; a named DSP or self-serve buying stack referenced in job descriptions. An attribution stack is the tell that a counted-delivery pitch will land rather than bounce.
- **Tier 3: Firmographics (20% Weight)**
    - **Employee Count (Range):** `50-800`
    - **Geography (Ideal):** `US headquartered, or US-revenue-primary with a named US marketing team`

**4. Disqualification Criteria (Anti-Signals)**

- **All media handled by an agency of record with no in-house buyer:** The decision does not sit in the building, so the qualifying question fails on the first call. The account belongs in ICP 1 as an agency relationship instead.
- **Annual digital media spend below roughly $2M:** A $50,000 commitment would be a disproportionate share of their year, which makes them a tester rather than a partner and makes a makegood commercially meaningless to them.
- **Betting, daily fantasy, sportsbook and casino:** Declined outright on every path. A policy exclusion, not a fit judgement.
- **Equity trading, brokerage and consumer investing apps:** Declined as direct competitors for InPlay's own account holders, now and more so after qualification.
- **No plausible football-following customer:** Where the product's customer has no sports crossover on any reading, no price fixes the mismatch. This is not a gender filter; no gender split is claimed in either direction.
- **Brands under active regulatory enforcement or live category controversy:** A pre-qualification company with counsel gating every public word cannot absorb imported reputational risk.

**5. Archetype-to-Pain Mapping (Sharpened for BEING PRICED OUT)**

Every signal below is evidence that the brand is already spending against football and has nothing distinctive to spend it on.

| Observable Signal (from Tiers 1 & 2) | Primary Inferred Pain (The Core Problem) | Relevance to the Offer (Why We Win) |
|---|---|---|
| Seasonal football creative on owned social in August and September, with no paid live-game presence anywhere | **`My biggest cultural moment of the year is a content calendar.`** The season is the one window when their customer is most reachable and most expensive. The plan for it is organic content, a couple of ambassador posts and a sweepstake, because everything that would place the brand inside the game is priced for national advertisers. Every year they watch the season happen next to them. | Entry is $50,000 inside the game itself: sponsored positions on the pages the account holder is trading on, plus field naming rights that render in live play and in every simulation. It converts a content calendar into a bought position at a price this buyer can clear. |
| An open in-house media or paid media role on the careers page or LinkedIn | **`The money is in this building and so is the blame.`** An in-house buyer owns the plan personally. There is no agency to route a bad call through and no third party to blame for a channel that underdelivers. That is why they can approve $50,000 in a month, and equally why they will not approve anything whose downside they cannot state in one sentence. | The downside is one sentence: a contracted quantity of exposures, and a shortfall either extended or delivered in a following period at no extra cost. That is the entire risk statement, and it is why this ICP can transact at the speed the offer needs. |
| Sports podcast host reads with unique promo codes, but no owned sports property anywhere on the site | **`I am buying sports-adjacent ears and calling it a sports plan.`** They have proven they will pay for football-literate audiences and have chosen the only surface their budget clears. The listener is commuting, the attention is borrowed, the attribution is a promo-code inference they defend quarterly, and every show that works already carries a competitor in the adjacent slot. | The same direct-buy muscle pointed at a session where the person is acting rather than listening, and where inventory keeps generating between games because the labs replay and simulate them. It is an upgrade to an existing behaviour, not a new one to learn. |
| An attribution stack visible (Triple Whale, Northbeam, Rockerbox) alongside connected TV or streaming sports activity | **`I can measure everything except the part of my plan I most want to defend.`** Their dashboards resolve performance media to the dollar, then stop dead at the brand and sports lines, which are reported in modelled reach nobody in the building trusts. They are asked every quarter what the sports money did, and the honest answer is a number they did not produce. | Delivery is reported as counted exposures against a KYC verified, age gated audience, with a weekly report through the flight. It speaks the language their stack already speaks, and it is the first sports line on the plan that reconciles. |
| A funding round, retail expansion or named US second-half push in the last 18 months | **`I have been funded to become known and I cannot buy the surfaces that do it.`** New capital or new shelf space arrives with an awareness mandate, and the obvious lever is broadcast-shaped media priced for companies ten times their size. They are caught between performance channels that do not build fame and fame channels they cannot afford. | Field naming rights are the stadium they could never buy, for a whole season, at a price the finance side will approve. It gives an in-house buyer a defensible "we got there first" line for the internal deck without a season-scale commitment. |

## ICP 3 · The Locked-Out Category Brand

**1. ICP Archetype Name:** `Locked-Out Category Brand - The Route-Denied Insider`

**2. Core Thesis:** A consumer brand whose customer is precisely this audience, financially active, mobile-first and sports-following, but which has no premium route to reach them. The cause splits two ways and the pain is identical: either no such route exists, which is the position of banks, fintech, financial media and content providers, or the mainstream platforms restrict the category outright, which is the position of crypto and web3. In both cases the decision is driven personally by a chief executive or chief marketing officer, frequently someone who trades or follows sport themselves, so they understand the proposition without explanation and can move in days rather than quarters. InPlay has one thread of evidence here, a set of financial-institution conversations that run through a chief executive who wants the brand in front of this audience and needs no education on why. It is one thread, not a pattern, and this ICP remains a hypothesis. Their dominant emotional pain is **HAVING NO ROUTE IN: their exact customer assembles somewhere they are structurally unable to buy.**

**3. Rubric-Aligned Profile**

- **Tier 1: Specific Business Type (50% Weight)**
    - **Primary Industry:** Finance-adjacent consumer brands, and restricted or alternative-category consumer brands.
    - **Ideal Verticals:** Banks, neobanks and money apps; payments and consumer fintech including buy-now-pay-later and rewards; financial media, research and market data; trading education and investor tools; crypto exchanges and web3 consumer, subject to the category ruling; content providers, streaming and sports media; insurance and personal finance.
    - **Defining Characteristics & Offerings (The "Idiot's Guide"):**
        - **Core Identity:** A consumer brand whose customer overlaps with a financially active, sports-following audience, where marketing is led from the top and the founder or chief executive is visibly involved in brand decisions.
        - **Website Evidence:** Compliance-heavy footers, licensing notices and disclaimers; a sponsorships or partnerships page, often featuring properties outside the US mainstream; aggressive referral or promotional offers signalling direct-response economics; app store badges above the fold.
    - **State Modifiers (Observable Signals of this Archetype):**
        - **GTM Motion:** Hiring Sponsorship Manager, Partnerships Lead (US), Head of Brand or US Growth Lead; press about US market entry, new state licences or a funded US push.
        - **Marketing Motion:** Saturation presence in the channels that will take them, podcast host reads, YouTube integrations and affiliate networks; sponsorships of properties outside US mainstream sport, which demonstrate both budget and sports conviction; a chief executive who posts publicly and often about markets or sport.
        - **Growth Signal:** A funding, profitability or token event creating a war chest; announced US expansion or licensing wins; a category rival making a sports move.
- **Tier 2: Business Model Category (30% Weight)**
    - **Primary Model:** Direct-response consumer, subscription or transactional. Marketing-led acquisition with high lifetime value.
    - **Target Customer:** Financially active, mobile-first, 18 to 55, follows sport.
    - **Tech Stack Signal:** Affiliate platforms such as Impact or PartnerStack; referral infrastructure; podcast attribution vendors; a mobile measurement partner such as AppsFlyer, Adjust or Branch.
- **Tier 3: Firmographics (20% Weight)**
    - **Employee Count (Range):** `50-5,000`
    - **Geography (Ideal):** `US headquartered, or global HQ with a named US market team and US permissions`

**4. Disqualification Criteria (Anti-Signals)**

- **Equity trading, brokerage and consumer investing apps:** Direct competitors for InPlay's own account holders, now and considerably more so after qualification. No position is for sale to them. The precise line between a prospect financial institution and a competitor one is `unknown` and sits with Edwin.
- **Betting, daily fantasy, sportsbook and casino:** Declined outright, every path, including programmatic.
- **No US permission or a stalled US entry:** If they cannot legally serve US customers, a US audience is unspendable for them.
- **Active regulatory enforcement or live scandal:** A pre-qualification company with counsel gating public language cannot absorb imported reputational risk.
- **Pure affiliate-arbitrage operators:** Thin skins over affiliate economics buy clicks, not positions, and carry no sponsorship budget or brand ambition.
- **No reachable chief executive or chief marketing officer:** This ICP converts on personal conviction at the top. If the decision sits three layers down, it is an ICP 2 motion, not this one.

**5. Archetype-to-Pain Mapping (Sharpened for HAVING NO ROUTE IN)**

Every signal below is evidence of budget and audience conviction colliding with a closed door.

| Observable Signal (from Tiers 1 & 2) | Primary Inferred Pain (The Core Problem) | Relevance to the Offer (Why We Win) |
|---|---|---|
| Documented platform restrictions on the category, or a compliance-heavy footer with licensing notices and state exclusions | **`I have real budget I am not allowed to spend where my customer is.`** Their media plan is defined by what is permitted rather than what works. Campaigns get rejected mid-flight and accounts suspended, and the team spends as much energy on policy navigation as on marketing. Strategy is whatever is left after the rules. | InPlay is willing premium inventory in front of their exact customer, with age gating and geo gating built in. A contracted position cannot be revoked by an algorithm mid-season. For a restricted brand, willing inventory is rarer than cheap inventory. |
| Sponsorships of properties outside US mainstream sport, or of minor properties inside it | **`I am paying sports-property prices for the wrong audience.`** They have proven they will pay for sport, but what is available to them is geographically or culturally wrong for a US growth mandate. The US sport they actually want will not take their category or their budget. Leadership sees the invoice and the flat US line in the same board pack. | InPlay delivers a US football audience without needing a league's category approval, and field naming rights deliver the prestige their existing sponsorships were bought for, pointed at the right country. |
| A chief executive visibly posting about markets, trading or sport | **`My brand belongs in this world and there is no door.`** The person who signs is personally inside this audience. They recognise the product in ninety seconds and are frustrated that no route exists. This is why these conversations move faster than any other and why the demo, not the deck, is the pitch. | No education is required. The decision sits with the person watching the demonstration, entry is $50,000, and it can be signed without a procurement cycle. Edwin and Cody have already seen exactly this pattern. |
| Affiliate and influencer-heavy acquisition, with referral or promo offers on the site | **`My acquisition channels embarrass my brand.`** Affiliates deliver volume with fraud and churn, and influencer reads are uneven and unscalable. They are a premium-priced product acquiring customers through bargain-bin channels because the premium channels will not have them. | A named position inside a KYC verified environment is a status purchase as much as a media one, and the audience is reached in a research and trading context rather than through a coupon. |
| An announced US expansion, new licences or a funded US push | **`The US mandate has a budget and nowhere to put it.`** Leadership has declared America the priority and funded it. The team owns an aggressive US target against a channel list that is mostly crossed out, and every quarter without a US flagship makes them look more peripheral. | InPlay's launch window aligns with their push. A season-long position across 142 days is a US beachhead they can point at, and the category is open to them where mainstream sport is not. |

## Gate

Run mechanically against the ICP guideline.

**Pass**
- Three groups, three genuinely distinct dominant pains. Sameness, priced out, no route in. No two are interchangeable.
- Buying group re-derived per ICP and it shifts cleanly along the axis: investment lead, in-house media buyer, chief executive.
- Every observable signal is verifiable from a website, a job post, a named tool or the press.
- Six disqualifiers per ICP, each explained.
- Five pain-mapping rows per ICP. Every "why we win" cell is grounded in something Section 2 of the offer actually claims.

**Flagged**
- ICP 2 and ICP 3 carry no deal evidence. Written as hypotheses.
- Crypto sits inside ICP 3's verticals and is not yet an approved category. Until offer gap 8 closes, crypto accounts are not worked.
- The line between a prospect financial institution and a competitor brokerage is `unknown`.

**Signals cut for failing the observability test:** "values innovation in media" and "has an underperforming agency relationship". Neither is checkable from outside, and a rubric that scores them scores noise.
