## Section 1: WHAT IT IS

InPlay Global sells advertising and sponsorship inside The InPlay Challenge 2026, a free, fully simulated NFL and college football trading competition delivered as a mobile app. What an advertiser touches is the app itself: sponsored positions on the pages an account holder uses to research, hold and trade positions in the companies behind football teams, plus naming rights on the Gamecast field and a programmatic unit in the header of every page. It is bought either on an insertion order, in which case InPlay runs the campaign, or as programmatic demand in the buyer's own DSP, in which case the buyer runs it. InPlay is pre-launch. No campaign has yet delivered. The competition period runs 142 days, from 22 August to the end of the NFL regular season.

**1. Literal description**

**Category** · In-app live sports media. Advertising inventory and sponsorship rights inside a live-event mobile application.

**Form factor** · Mobile app ad inventory, plus contracted sponsorship rights over named surfaces and the Gamecast field.

**Surface** · The InPlay Challenge mobile app. Premium pages, the Gamecast, the back test, simulation and strategy labs, and the header of every page.

**Delivery model** · Insertion order for sponsorship and rights. Programmatic through the buyer's own DSP for volume inventory.

**Operating model** · Splits by path. InPlay-operated on a direct insertion order. Buyer-operated on programmatic.

**Buying model** · **[S]** committed exposure quantity against a fixed price, tiered on engagement delivered. **[A]** consumed as delivered, priced per impression.

**Mechanism arc** · A verified account holder researches through the week and trades through live games and their simulations; sponsored positions and programmatic units on those surfaces generate counted exposures; the advertiser receives delivery reporting, with any shortfall made good in inventory.

**2. Why it exists**

**Underlying problem** · A brand that wants to be present inside live American football buys stadium, broadcast or league sponsorship at a price starting around one hundred thousand dollars and running to half a million for a single afternoon, or buys sports-adjacent audio and social where the person is right and the moment is wrong. There is no measured way to be inside the game itself at a price a mid-market advertiser can approve.

**What shifted** · Live sport attention moved onto a second screen the fan holds throughout the game. Prediction markets demonstrated over the last two years that there is real appetite to trade sport live. Both betting and prediction markets are outcome dependent, so the person acts once and leaves. InPlay is price-path dependent, so the person stays and trades across every play.

**Prior answers** · Connected TV and streaming sports, sports podcasts and host reads, social content around tentpoles, and real stadium or team sponsorship. Each delivers the right person at the wrong moment, or the right moment at a price set for national brands.

**3. What it is NOT**

**Not a gambling apparatus.**
**Confused because** it is live football, prices move, and people are trading through the game.
**Differs in** the competition is fully simulated with no real money at risk, there is no house, no book and no odds, and betting, daily fantasy, sportsbook and casino categories are declined outright, on every path including programmatic.

**Not a single-event outcome product.**
**Confused because** it sits in the same mental category as sports betting and prediction markets.
**Differs in** those open at kickoff and settle at the whistle. Here an account holder trades every play of every game across the whole season, can hold a position for six games or for the season, and can close at any time.

**Not a broadcast, stadium or league buy.**
**Confused because** it is live football inventory sold around game moments.
**Differs in** the sponsorship minimum is fifty thousand dollars, delivery is counted in exposures, and a shortfall is made good in inventory.

**Not a long-tail programmatic in-app buy.**
**Confused because** the volume units are standard in-app native and display.
**Differs in** the account holder is KYC verified at account level, the surfaces are declared and named, and the buyer knows what the person was doing when the impression served.

**4. The job it does**

**Primary job** · Places a brand beside a verified account holder while they hold and trade positions in football team companies, across every play of a 142-day season and every simulation of it.

**Secondary jobs**
- Gives a media buyer one fall sports line item that is not on their competitor's plan.
- Puts a brand on a field surface that real stadium and team inventory has priced them out of.
- Produces first-party delivery reporting against a verified audience rather than modelled reach.

## Section 2: HOW IT WORKS [media pack]

**Overview.** A verified account holder researches through the week and trades through live games and their simulations. Sponsored positions on named surfaces and programmatic units in the header and on volatility moments generate exposures. Those are counted, verified and reported, and any shortfall against a contracted sponsorship quantity is made good in inventory.

### [S] Sponsorship

Covers title sponsorship, premium page sponsorship and Gamecast field naming. The process is the same for all three. Title sponsorship is the top scope, not a different mechanism.

**Step · Present the placements and the price** · InPlay
InPlay shows the buyer which positions are available, how each can be bought, and what each costs. The output is a placement and price proposal naming specific surfaces.
**Contributes** · so the buyer is choosing from declared, named positions rather than an abstract audience.

**Step · Mock the creative onto the device** · InPlay
InPlay returns with a presentation showing samples of the advertising as it appears on the phone, in position on the real surface.
**Contributes** · so the buyer approves against the surface rather than a spec sheet, and nothing is discovered after launch.

**Step · Schedule the flight and load the delivery rules** · InPlay
The campaign period is set, full season or part. The exposure volume and the serving rules for every ad unit are loaded into the ad stack.
**Contributes** · so the contracted quantity and the serving rules exist in the stack before a single impression runs.

**Step · Traffic the creative** · InPlay
If the buyer's creative meets a common standard it is inserted as supplied. If it does not, InPlay builds it. Creative goes in before launch, or shortly after where the buyer needs speed.
**Contributes** · so a non-standard creative format does not delay or block the campaign.

**Step · Apply the field brand, where field naming is bought** · InPlay
Gamecast field naming differs from the other sponsorship scopes in one respect. The brand is applied once to the field and persists, rather than being trafficked as rotating creative. It renders in live games and inside the back test, simulation and strategy labs.
**Contributes** · so the sponsor holds a permanent named surface that also earns during research and simulation, when no live game is running.

**Step · Confirm the launch in writing** · InPlay
InPlay sends a launch confirmation covering what went live, how it went live, and the targeting applied.
**Contributes** · so the buyer holds a written record of the campaign as configured before delivery starts accruing against it.

**Step · Verify delivery** · InPlay ad ops
Google Tag Manager and Google Analytics verify the media placements and the ad serving through the flight. Independent viewability and invalid traffic verification through Open Measurement or a third-party vendor is `unknown` and outstanding.
**Contributes** · so delivered volume is measured rather than asserted.

**Step · Report weekly** · InPlay
InPlay pulls the statistics and sends a weekly performance report against the contracted quantity.
**Contributes** · so the buyer sees pacing while there is still time to act on it.

**Step · Settle at the end of the flight** · InPlay
InPlay confirms whether the contracted commitments were fulfilled. Where delivery falls short, the makegood in Section 7 applies.
**Contributes** · so a shortfall is settled on a stated mechanism rather than negotiated after the fact.

**Open step** · Applying a title sponsor's brand across InPlay's own outreach, marketing and landing pages has no named owner and no stated turnaround. `unknown`. Until it has an owner, agents may not promise off-app title presence.

### [A] Programmatic

**Step · Serve the header unit** · InPlay ad stack
A programmatic unit runs in the top right corner of the header on every page, native to the application, click-through to the advertiser.
**Contributes** · so there is always-on volume inventory independent of which surface the account holder is on.

**Step · Fill the volatility moment unit** · InPlay ad stack
When a significant moment occurs in a game, a branded unit appears on the field. There are 20 to 25 such moments per game. The unit is filled programmatically rather than sold as a premium placement, because the volume exceeds what direct sales can fill. Volatility moments fire in live games and in replays and simulations.
**Contributes** · so the highest-attention moments in the session are monetised at scale rather than left unfilled.

**Step · Serve video interstitials** · InPlay ad stack
Video interstitials are served in the app. Placement, length and frequency are `unknown` pending the launch inventory map.
**Contributes** · so a buyer's video budget has a defined place to go inside the session.

**Step · Transact through the buyer's DSP** · Buyer
The buyer runs the campaign in their own demand-side platform. InPlay accepts the demand through its SSP and ad server. Whether InPlay reports at all on this path is `unknown`.
**Contributes** · so a buyer whose agency will not process an insertion order with an unfamiliar publisher can still transact.

## Section 3: PAIN POINTS

The buyer's world is a fall plan mostly written in June that mostly looks like last year's. They own a number and they own a calendar. The surfaces that would genuinely distinguish the brand inside live football are priced for advertisers ten times their size, and everything else on the plan is available to any competitor with a similar budget. When something genuinely new does reach them, it arrives as a forecast they would have to personally underwrite.

**Buying-group roles**

**Media buyer or marketing manager** · economic buyer · optimises for delivered, defensible reach against a plan they can present upward.

**Brand safety and procurement** · blocker · optimises for the absence of category, context and contractual risk. Can also ask for a campaign to be paused once live.

Note: on a direct buy there is no practitioner role on the buyer's side. InPlay's ad specialist runs the campaign. That is an advantage, not an omission.

**Pain 1 · "You want me to buy an audience that does not exist yet."** *(dominant)*
**Situation** · InPlay is pre-launch. The buyer is being asked to commit against an audience that has not arrived, with no delivery history, no benchmarks and no case study. Edwin and Cody report this is the most common reason a conversation goes quiet, and it is stated directly: they want an audience, and there is not one yet.
**Impact, media buyer** · they cannot construct an internal case out of a projection, so the opportunity dies in the inbox rather than in a meeting.
**Trigger events** · a plan lock date, a quarterly reforecast.
**Addressed by** · Section 2 [S] schedule and settle steps · Section 4 outcome 1 · Section 7 makegood.

**Pain 2 · "Everything that would make my fall plan distinctive is priced for brands ten times my size."**
**Situation** · A Saturday or Sunday sponsorship in college football or the NFL starts around one hundred thousand dollars and runs to half a million, and buys a single afternoon. Real stadium and field signage is locked up by national brands on multi-year deals.
**Impact, media buyer** · they cannot answer "what is new this year" with anything the finance side would call a media decision.
**Addressed by** · Section 2 [S] · Section 4 outcomes 2 and 9 · Section 7 fifty thousand dollar minimum.

**Pain 3 · "Sports-adjacent is the honest description of everything I buy."**
**Situation** · Podcasts and sports social reach the right person while they are commuting or scrolling, not while the game is on. The buyer has bought the audience without buying the attention, and the difference does not show up in any number they report.
**Impact, media buyer** · reach is reported, attention is assumed, and the gap between them is the part of the plan they cannot defend.
**Addressed by** · Section 2 [S] premium page and field steps · Section 5 claim 2.

**Pain 4 · "In-app display reads as inflated until somebody shows me the refresh rules."**
**Situation** · The buyer has been burned by in-app inventory with undeclared refresh, stacked units and invalid traffic. Their default assumption about a new app publisher is that the counts are inflated. InPlay reports this is not currently a common objection, but it is a standing one in the category and it is raised at the trafficking stage.
**Impact, brand safety** · absent declared refresh rules and independent verification, the publisher does not clear.
**Addressed by** · Section 2 [S] verify step · Section 5 claim 4. Currently under-answered. See the gaps list.

**Pain 5 · "The category sounds like betting, and I cannot get betting past brand safety."**
**Situation** · Live football, moving prices and trading reads as a sportsbook to anyone who has not seen the product. Many brands hold a standing prohibition on gambling adjacency, and the buyer will not spend political capital arguing a definition. Edwin and Cody confirm this is the first thing a buyer thinks.
**Impact, brand safety** · rejects on the description alone unless the distinction is made concrete and early.
**Impact, media buyer** · will not champion something they expect to lose internally.
**Addressed by** · Section 1 what it is NOT · Section 4 outcome 5.

**Pain 6 · "My agency will not process a custom insertion order with a publisher they have never heard of."**
**Situation** · Even where the buyer wants the campaign, the operational path runs through an agency that transacts in its own DSP and treats a bespoke insertion order from an unknown publisher as a compliance and finance problem.
**Impact, media buyer** · the deal stalls in trafficking rather than in strategy.
**Addressed by** · Section 2 [A] DSP step · Section 4 outcome 6.

**Pain 7 · "My video money has nowhere premium to go that is not CTV at CTV prices."**
**Situation** · Video budget is up, the premium sports supply is connected TV, and CTV sports pricing plus frequency capping leaves the buyer paying a high rate for fragmented reach they cannot connect to anything downstream.
**Impact, media buyer** · they carry a video line that must be spent and a shrinking list of places to spend it.
**Addressed by** · Section 2 [A] video interstitial step · Section 4 outcome 7.

**Pain 8 · "My fall plan is the same as last year, and the same as my competitor's."**
**Situation** · The fall plan is CTV, sports audio, social around tentpoles and a creator or two. Every line on it is available to any competitor with a similar budget.
**Impact, media buyer** · differentiation has to come from creative, because it cannot come from the media.
**Addressed by** · Section 2 [S] · Section 4 outcome 8 · Section 5 claim 3.

## Section 5: WHY US vs THE STATUS QUO

A buyer who wants live football attention this autumn has five real options, and four of them are things they are already doing.

**Alternative · Connected TV and streaming sports**
**Why buyers choose it** · it is the closest thing to broadcast a mid-market budget can enter, bought through paths finance already accepts.
**Where it falls short** · pricing is set by national demand, frequency capping fragments reach, and the measurement rarely connects to anything the buyer can act on afterwards.

**Alternative · Sports podcasts and audio host reads**
**Why buyers choose it** · it reaches an unambiguously football-literate audience through a trusted voice, at a price a mid-market brand can clear.
**Where it falls short** · the listener is commuting or doing something else, attribution is promo-code inference, and the best shows already carry a competitor.

**Alternative · Real stadium, team and league sponsorship**
**Why buyers choose it** · it is the actual game and it carries genuine prestige.
**Where it falls short** · a hundred thousand to half a million dollars for a single afternoon, and the good inventory is held by national brands on multi-year deals.

**Alternative · Open programmatic in-app sports inventory**
**Why buyers choose it** · it is cheap, instantly available, and needs no new counterparty.
**Where it falls short** · refresh behaviour is usually undeclared, session context is unknown, and the buyer cannot say what the person was doing when the impression served.

**Alternative · Doing nothing new this season**
**Why buyers choose it** · nobody is fired for running last year's plan, and a new publisher costs internal credibility to approve.
**Where it falls short** · the plan stays interchangeable with the competition's, and the buyer has no answer to "what is new".

**Claim 1 · The advertiser is not underwriting the audience forecast. InPlay is.**
**Versus** · all five.
**Substance** · a sponsorship contracts a quantity of exposures, and a shortfall is made good in inventory at no additional cost. No alternative on the list commits an outcome quantity to a buyer at this level, because none of them needs to: they have delivery history and InPlay does not.
**Barrier countered** · "you have no audience yet and I would be the one carrying that."

**Claim 2 · The advertising serves while the person is acting, not while they are watching.**
**Versus** · CTV, podcasts.
**Substance** · the sponsored positions run on the surfaces an account holder is using to research through the week and trade through the game. That is a structural property of the surface, not a claim about creative.
**Barrier countered** · "everything I buy is sports-adjacent."

**Claim 3 · The inventory is not bounded by the live game.**
**Versus** · all five.
**Substance** · the back test, simulation and strategy labs replay and simulate games. An account holder may run a game many times before watching it live, and the field brand and volatility moment units render in those sessions as well as in live play. No broadcast, stadium, podcast or CTV property generates inventory when the game is not on.
**Barrier countered** · "there are only so many game hours in a season."

**Claim 4 · The audience is verified at account level, not inferred.**
**Versus** · open programmatic, podcasts.
**Substance** · account holders complete KYC and are age gated and geo gated to eligible US jurisdictions, so age and location are properties of the account rather than probabilistic segments.
**Barrier countered** · "who is actually seeing this."

**Claim 5 · The field itself is for sale.**
**Versus** · stadium, team and league sponsorship.
**Substance** · field naming rights are applied once and persist for the season, in live games and in every simulation. Real field and stadium signage of equivalent prominence is not available to this buyer at this price, in this season, on any comparable term.
**Barrier countered** · "I could never afford to be in a stadium."

**Claim 6 · It transacts the way the buyer's agency already transacts.**
**Versus** · stadium, team and league sponsorship, and bespoke sports properties.
**Substance** · the same demand can run programmatically in the buyer's own DSP, or on an insertion order where rights and exclusivity are involved. Traditional sports properties transact only one way.
**Barrier countered** · "my agency will not onboard you."

## Section 7: COMMERCIAL SHAPE [context, not quotable by default]

Sponsorship is bought against a committed quantity of exposures, at a fixed price, tiered on the engagement delivered. Programmatic is consumed as it delivers and priced per impression. Both paths run over the same 142-day season.

### [S] Sponsorship

**Pricing** · Fixed price against a committed exposure quantity, tiered on engagement delivered.

**Price points** · Minimum buy fifty thousand dollars. Entire season minimum one hundred and fifty thousand dollars.

**Commitment** · Full season, or part of the season. Terms, notice and renewal are `unknown`.

**Entry path** · A demonstration of the app and the surfaces, one recommended package, then an insertion order.

**Risk reversal** · Drafted and agreed in session, subject to counsel sign-off before it is quoted:

> "If InPlay delivers fewer than the contracted exposures within the campaign period, InPlay will, at the advertiser's election, either extend the current flight until the contracted quantity is delivered, or deliver the shortfall in a following competition period. In each case at no additional cost. Cash refunds are not offered."

Two definitions must sit under this clause before it is used, or it is unenforceable. First, what an exposure means: served, viewable or completed, defined separately for display and video. Second, whose numbers settle a dispute, InPlay's ad server or the buyer's verification vendor. Both are `unknown`.

**Quotability** · `unknown`. Nobody has ruled on whether agents may lead with the makegood in a first touch. Default is held for the meeting.

### [A] Programmatic

**Price points** · Around four dollars fifty to five dollars CPM. This is the negotiated rate, not a rate card. A published rate card above it, with room to discount down, does not exist yet and is outstanding.

**Commitment** · None. No committed quantity.

**Entry path** · Programmatic demand through the buyer's own DSP, accepted through InPlay's SSP and ad server.

**Risk reversal** · None. Consumed as delivered.
