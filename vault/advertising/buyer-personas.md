# ICP 1 · The Global Agency Investment Desk

**Dominant pain inherited by both personas below:** SAMENESS. Paid to find what nobody else has, handed the same thing in new packaging.

**Buying group:** the investment or innovation lead owns the agency-level decision to adopt a property at all. The account-side investment director or planner decides whether it reaches a specific client, and is the gatekeeper whose recommendation moves everything.

---

## Persona 1.1 · Head of Investment / Head of Innovation (Economic Buyer for the agency relationship)

**1.0 Archetype Name**
The Edge Broker

**2.0 Role & Functional Identity**

- **2.1 Primary Function:** Owns the agency's relationship with new media and sponsorship partners and the decision to adopt a property across clients. The person who turns "an idea one planner liked" into "a property this agency brings to everybody".
- **2.2 Core Responsibilities:**
    - Owns investment strategy, partner relationships and the innovation or emerging-media agenda
    - Decides which new partners and properties the agency adopts and promotes to clients
    - Negotiates agency-level commercial terms: rates, revenue share, exclusivity of access
    - Carries differentiation into new-business pitches and major client business reviews
    - Protects the agency's margin model and its standing with clients and holding-company leadership
    - Sets the rule of engagement with partners, including how direct and agency deals coexist
- **2.3 Scope, Seniority & Authority:** **Scope** practice or agency, across many accounts · **Seniority** executive or senior director · **Decision Authority** owns the agency-side relationship and can commit the agency to adopt and promote a property. The per-client spend is still the client's money, but this person decides whether InPlay is brought to clients at all.

**3.0 Psychographic Profile**

- **Identity & Self-Image:** A market-maker and a tastemaker. Sees themselves as the person who spots the next channel before the holding company does and turns it into a competitive weapon for the whole firm.
- **Core Values / What They Optimise For:** Agency differentiation in pitches; portfolio economics, meaning a property that scales across many clients rather than one; being demonstrably first among rival agencies; protecting client trust.
- **How They Measure Their Own Worth:** Pitches won on differentiation they sourced, the number of accounts a new property gets placed across, and whether holding-company leadership points at their practice as ahead of the market.
- **Worldview & Biases Toward This Category:** Adopts new properties as a deliberate strategy, but only ones that are genuinely exclusive, defensible to clients, and structured so the agency is not disintermediated. Deeply wary of partners who court the agency for introductions and then go direct.

**4.0 A Day in the Life**
The morning is a new-business review, and the strategy lead needs a differentiator the incumbent agency cannot match by Friday. Mid-morning, two partners pitch first-to-market properties; one is interesting and one is the metaverse wearing a new hat. Then a holding-company call about principal-based media and client trust, which is a reminder that opaque deals are under scrutiny and clean, ownable ones are gold. Lunch is with a key client's marketing lead, who asks, pointedly, what this agency can get them that the last one could not. The afternoon is portfolio thinking: which of the agency's twenty accounts a new sports property could serve, and how to structure terms so the agency wins on every placement rather than being thanked after the introduction. They end the day writing the same note they wrote last quarter, which is that the practice is judged on being first and the market has not produced a genuine first in nine months.

**5.0 Pains & Fears**

- **5.1 Operational Pains:**
    - **"Differentiation has a half-life and mine is expiring."** "Whatever made us special last year is table stakes now. Retail media, connected TV, creators, all of it. I am permanently one cycle away from looking like everybody else."
    - **"I am pitched a hundred firsts a year and three are real."** "Sorting the genuine from the repackaged is most of my job, and the cost of adopting a dud is not money, it is the firm's credibility with a client."
    - **"Most partners who court me want my clients, not me."** "They use the agency for the introduction and then go direct. I have been burned and I remember exactly who did it."
- **5.2 Strategic & Political Pains:**
    - **"We win and lose multimillion-dollar accounts on the idea the client has not seen."** "Pitch differentiation is existential here, and ownable inventory is the scarcest kind of all. Everything else is a price conversation."
    - **"If I put the agency behind a platform that flops in front of a client, that is the agency's reputation and it is mine."** "Adoption is a bet with the whole firm's credibility, not a line item."
    - **"Leadership wants us ahead of the market and clients want us auditable."** "I am squeezed between being innovative and being defensible in a procurement review, and those two pull in opposite directions."
- **5.3 The Deepest Fear:** That the agency becomes a commodity buying desk, undercut on price and bypassed by clients going direct or in-house, with nothing proprietary left to justify the fee, and that the person who was supposed to prevent that was them.

**6.0 What They've Already Tried (and Why It Disappointed)**

- **Exclusive-access deals with platforms and publishers:** Sometimes real, often exclusive in name only, and undercut later when the platform went direct to advertisers anyway.
- **Building an in-house innovation practice:** Generates thought leadership and a full meeting calendar, but the bottleneck was never the team. It is the supply of genuinely new, ownable inventory.
- **Sports and entertainment partnerships:** Strong story value, but bespoke, hard to scale across clients, and hard to defend to procurement on price.
- **Principal-based and rebated media as a margin source:** Increasingly under client scrutiny. The trust cost now outweighs the margin, which pushes them toward clean, ownable deals.

**7.0 Decision Criteria & Objections**

- **What they weigh:**
    - Exclusivity of access: can the agency be first, and stay differentiated, for a meaningful window
    - Scalability across clients: how many accounts this can serve, and the portfolio economics of that
    - Channel protection: a rule of engagement that keeps the agency in the deal rather than bypassed
    - Defensibility: clean, transparent, measurable terms that survive a client's procurement review
- **Objections:**
    - "What stops you going direct to my clients once we have made the introductions?"
    - "You have not launched. I would be putting the agency's credibility behind a forecast."
    - "Is this exclusive to us, or will my rival agency have it next quarter?"
    - "How does this get booked, and does it survive a client's procurement audit?"

**8.0 Trigger Events (Why Now)**
- A major pitch in flight where a differentiator could decide the account
- A holding-company mandate to lead on innovation or emerging media this year
- A key client demanding the young sports audience across their plan
- A rival agency publicly winning on a proprietary property
- Annual partner and investment planning, when agency-level deals for the season are struck

**9.0 Information Diet & Trusted Voices**
- Agency and ad-industry trade press (Ad Age, Adweek, Digiday, Campaign) and analyst commentary on agency models
- Holding-company leadership and peer investment or innovation leads at rival agencies
- The clients themselves, whose demands shape what differentiation is worth sourcing
- Sponsorship and media-intelligence data such as SponsorUnited, to benchmark what a deal should look like
- Industry stages, Cannes, Possible, Advertising Week and the 4A's, where being first is a public flex

**10.0 Success Metrics (What "Good" Looks Like)**
- From differentiation with a short half-life, to a property the agency holds first across its accounts
- From partners who bypass the agency, to a relationship where the agency is in every placement
- From bespoke one-off sponsorships, to something placeable across many accounts at a $50,000 entry
- From margin under procurement scrutiny, to a clean contracted position with a stated delivery commitment

**11.0 The Message That Lands**
Lead with the thing that does not exist anywhere else, then the portfolio maths. "Your sports platform sells you the game's audience. InPlay sells the audience that cannot look away: verified fans researching all week, trading every snap, all game long. It is a football market nobody has advertised in, because it did not exist until this season. Field naming rights, sponsored positions inside the trading session, and a programmatic path through your own DSP. Entry is $50,000, so it places across a roster rather than sitting on one account. We are pre-launch and we carry that risk, not your client: the exposure quantity is contracted and any shortfall is delivered later or the flight extended, at no extra cost." Do not oversell the audience. This person has heard a hundred forecasts and will respect the one seller who says the number does not exist yet and hands them the mechanism instead.

**12.0 Example Titles**
Chief Investment Officer · Head of Investment · Head of Innovation · EVP Strategic Partnerships · Managing Director of Investment · Head of Emerging Media

## Persona 1.2 · Investment Director / Media Planner on the account (Champion and Executor)

**1.0 Archetype Name**
The Plan-Builder Hunting an Edge

**2.0 Role & Functional Identity**

- **2.1 Primary Function:** Builds and places the media plan for one or more brand accounts. The person who decides which inventory makes the plan, and who then traffics, paces and reports it.
- **2.2 Core Responsibilities:**
    - Builds channel plans and budgets for assigned client accounts
    - Evaluates and recommends new inventory and partners to the account lead and the client
    - Negotiates placements and manages trafficking, pacing and optimisation
    - Reports campaign performance back to the client in the client's own metrics
    - Protects the agency's margin and the cleanliness of the buy
    - Gates what reaches the client, so a recommendation from them is most of the battle
- **2.3 Scope, Seniority & Authority:** **Scope** account or team, one to several client accounts · **Seniority** manager, senior manager or director · **Decision Authority** does not own the client's budget and rarely commits a season alone. Can commit small test budgets within a plan, and is the gatekeeper whose recommendation moves both the client and the agency lead.

**3.0 Psychographic Profile**

- **Identity & Self-Image:** A craftsperson of the plan, who knows the channel landscape better than the client and better than the rival agency. Quietly collects "I brought that to them first" wins and remembers every one.
- **Core Values / What They Optimise For:** Client results they can report cleanly, plans that make them look smart in a room, and ease of execution, because anything that traffics painlessly is worth a premium to them.
- **How They Measure Their Own Worth:** Whether their accounts renew and grow, whether the client repeats their ideas back to them, and whether they are seen internally as the person who finds the good stuff.
- **Worldview & Biases Toward This Category:** Curious about new inventory and wary of putting an unproven publisher in front of a client and owning the fallout. Needs turnkey specifications, a clean measurement story, and reassurance that the agency is not being cut out.

**4.0 A Day in the Life**
The morning is pacing reports and a client email asking why a line is flat and what is new for next quarter. Mid-morning, three reps pitch inventory: two are programmatic in a new wrapper and one is a fan-engagement idea recycled from 2022. They take all three, because the client wants novelty and the cupboard is bare. Lunch is internal, and the account lead wants a differentiated idea for a quarterly business review, where "more of the same, optimised" will not survive the room. The afternoon is trafficking and a measurement reconciliation that will not tie out, which eats two hours and produces nothing anybody will thank them for. By late afternoon they are building next quarter's plan and the channel mix looks exactly like last quarter's, which looks exactly like the plan the rival agency showed the same client in June.

**5.0 Pains & Fears**

- **5.1 Operational Pains:**
    - **"Every plan I build looks like the last one."** "Same networks, same upfronts, same exchanges. I am optimising sameness and writing the word strategy at the top of it."
    - **"Most new-inventory meetings waste my afternoon."** "Reps promise exclusive and premium and it resolves to rebrokered programmatic I am already buying cheaper. I sit through it out of obligation."
    - **"Specs and trafficking are where good ideas die."** "If I cannot get creative specs, a clean tag and a reporting export, it does not matter how interesting the idea is. I will not be the person explaining a broken tag to a client."
- **5.2 Strategic & Political Pains:**
    - **"The client expects novelty I cannot manufacture."** "Every review needs a what-is-new slide, and the market keeps handing me the same thing in new packaging."
    - **"If I recommend it and it flops, it is my client relationship that takes the hit."** "I am the one in the room when they ask why we tried it. Nobody upstairs is in that room."
    - **"If I bring something and the agency gets cut out, that is on me."** "I am not going to introduce a partner who then goes direct to my client and disintermediates us."
- **5.3 The Deepest Fear:** Becoming an order-taker who reskins the same plan every quarter until the client decides they can buy it themselves, or a system can, and the role quietly disappears.

**6.0 What They've Already Tried (and Why It Disappointed)**

- **Innovation and emerging-media vendor meetings:** A constant stream, mostly repackaged programmatic. The genuine finds are rare enough that scepticism became the default posture.
- **Sponsorship and experiential one-offs:** Memorable, hard to measure, hard to repeat, priced as bespoke, and difficult to defend to a procurement-minded client.
- **Programmatic private marketplaces sold as premium:** Cleaner than the open exchange but not differentiated. The rival agency buys the same deal identifiers.
- **Connected TV and retail media as the new budget:** Real, and now table stakes. Everybody's plan has them, so they no longer win anything.

**7.0 Decision Criteria & Objections**

- **What they weigh:**
    - Differentiation: is this genuinely new, or will the rival agency have it next month
    - Ease of execution: creative specs, trafficking path, and a measurement story they can report from
    - Channel protection: does the agency stay in the deal with its margin intact
    - Client fit: which of their accounts this actually suits, and how much of the roster it can serve
- **Objections:**
    - "What are the creative specifications and how does this traffic? If the answer is bespoke, I am out."
    - "How do I report this in the client's metrics, and who verifies the delivery?"
    - "An unlaunched publisher in front of my client is my risk, not yours."
    - "Are you going to go around me to my client?"

**8.0 Trigger Events (Why Now)**
- A quarterly business review or renewal is coming and the what-is-new slide is empty
- A new client win in a football-relevant category lands on their desk
- A competitor agency just won a pitch on a differentiated property
- The client explicitly asked for the young sports audience and the cupboard is bare
- Fall planning is underway and they need a season idea inside two weeks

**9.0 Information Diet & Trusted Voices**
- Agency trade press, Ad Age, Adweek, Digiday, Campaign, read for what is coming rather than for opinion
- Internal investment and innovation leads, whose blessing they need before recommending anything
- Peer planners and buyers at other agencies, where "is this real?" gets answered candidly and fast
- Ad operations and programmatic communities where specs, refresh and verification are argued in detail
- Conference floors, where being first to a property is the flex that gets repeated

**10.0 Success Metrics (What "Good" Looks Like)**
- From a photocopied plan, to one line no rival agency can match
- From more-of-the-same-optimised, to a review slide the client repeats to their own boss
- From bespoke, unmeasurable sponsorships, to a position that reports weekly against a contracted number
- From fear of being bypassed, to a protected relationship placing positions across the roster

**11.0 The Message That Lands**
Front-load the two things this person screens for, which are execution and exposure. "InPlay is a free NFL and college football trading competition. Verified fans research all week and trade every snap, all game long, and the labs mean they replay and simulate games between fixtures too. Sponsored positions sit on the pages they are trading on, and it can run as programmatic demand in your own DSP, so there is no bespoke insertion order to push through finance. We are pre-launch, so the exposure quantity is contracted and any shortfall is extended or delivered in a following period at no extra cost. Fifteen minutes and I will show you the screen and exactly where the brand sits." Name the DSP path early, because it removes the objection this persona raises first. Never lead with the audience size, because there is not one yet and they will notice.

**12.0 Example Titles**
Investment Director · Digital Investment Director · Senior Media Planner · Associate Director of Investment · Group Director, Media · Connections Planner

# ICP 2 · The Priced-Out Brand, buying direct

**Dominant pain inherited by both personas below:** BEING PRICED OUT. The one place their customer is most reachable starts at six figures for a single afternoon.

**Buying group:** the in-house media owner signs and runs it. The sports marketing or brand partnerships lead is the person who goes looking for something like this and drags the media owner into the room, but usually cannot sign.

---

## Persona 2.1 · Director or Head of Media, in-house (Economic Buyer)

**1.0 Archetype Name**
The Plan Owner With Nothing New To Put In It

**2.0 Role & Functional Identity**

- **2.1 Primary Function:** Owns the brand's paid media plan end to end, in house. What gets bought, from whom, at what price, and what it is reported to have done. The only person in the company who can put a new publisher on the plan, and the only one who has to explain it afterwards.
- **2.2 Core Responsibilities:**
    - Builds and defends the annual and quarterly media plan across paid social, search, connected TV, audio and in-app
    - Negotiates directly with publishers and platforms, without an agency of record for digital
    - Owns the fall football campaign: budget, surfaces, flight dates, and creative delivered against them
    - Runs the measurement story: what is reported upward, in what units, against what comparison
    - Manages one to four paid and growth people plus a contract ad operations resource
    - Personally sponsors any new channel test, and personally carries it when it fails
- **2.3 Scope, Seniority & Authority:** **Scope** department, a media function of two to six inside a marketing organisation of ten to sixty · **Seniority** senior manager to director, occasionally VP · **Decision Authority** can commit $25,000 to $100,000 from the test or innovation line on their own signature within a quarter. Above roughly $150,000 needs the marketing lead, which they can usually secure in two to three weeks with a one-page rationale.

**3.0 Psychographic Profile**

- **Identity & Self-Image:** A practitioner rather than a strategist. Proud of being the person who actually knows what things cost and what they delivered, in a company where most opinions about media are held by people who have never bought any.
- **Core Values / What They Optimise For:** Defensibility first. Then speed, because a slow decision is the same as a no. Then not being the last brand in the category to find something.
- **How They Measure Their Own Worth:** Whether the number they promised came in, whether the plan survived contact with finance, and whether anything on it this year is theirs rather than inherited.
- **Worldview & Biases Toward This Category:** Has sat through dozens of new-inventory pitches and approved perhaps two. Believes a stated delivery commitment is the only thing a pre-launch publisher can say that means anything, and becomes visibly more interested the moment one appears. Allergic to the word opportunity in a subject line.

**4.0 A Day in the Life**
The morning starts in a spreadsheet reconciling last week's delivery against three platform dashboards that disagree with each other by four per cent, and an email from finance asking why the connected TV line invoiced above plan. By ten there is a call with an audio seller wanting a fifteen per cent renewal increase against listenership that has not moved, who says the word premium eleven times. Midday is the fall campaign review: the creative is good, the plan behind it is connected TV, two podcast shows, a creator package and organic content, and when the marketing lead asks what is new this year the honest answer is the creator package, which the brand two doors down announced in June. The afternoon is trafficking, where specs bounce and a tag fires twice and the ad operations contractor is on another client until Thursday. At six they watch a game with the phone in their hand, notice they have not looked at the television in nine minutes, and think, without doing anything about it, that this is where the money should be going.

**5.0 Pains & Fears**

- **5.1 Operational Pains:**
    - **"Nothing on my plan is mine."** "I inherited most of it and renewed the rest. If I got hit by a bus the plan would run itself, and that is not a compliment."
    - **"I spend more time reconciling delivery than deciding anything."** "Three dashboards, three numbers, one invoice, and finance wants the difference explained by Thursday. By the time I have proved what happened, the window to change what happens next has closed."
    - **"Every seller says premium and none of them will tell me what I actually get."** "I ask what the position is, where it sits, and what I will be able to see afterwards. Most of them go quiet on at least one of the three."
- **5.2 Strategic & Political Pains:**
    - **"Football is the one moment we should own and my plan for it is a content calendar."** "Our customer is watching sixteen weekends of football and our presence is two ambassador posts and a sweepstake, because everything that would actually put us in the game is priced for a company ten times our size."
    - **"I have one new-thing credit a year and I have to spend it well."** "If the next new channel misses, I am the person who does that, and it follows me into every planning conversation for two years."
    - **"Brand safety will kill anything that sounds like gambling and I will not spend capital arguing."** "It does not matter whether the distinction is real. If I have to spend three meetings explaining why this is not a sportsbook, I have already lost more than the campaign is worth."
- **5.3 The Deepest Fear:** That they become a very good administrator of somebody else's plan, competent and reconciled and entirely replaceable, and that when the brand finally does something interesting in media it is because somebody else brought it in.

**6.0 What They've Already Tried (and Why It Disappointed)**

- **Connected TV and streaming sports:** The closest thing to being in the game that they could actually buy. It delivered at a rate that hurt, frequency capped into fragmented reach, and reported in a way that never connected to anything downstream. Renewed anyway, because nothing else fits the slot.
- **Sports podcast host reads at scale:** Worked for two seasons, then saturated. Every show that converts now carries a competitor, attribution is promo-code archaeology, and renewals arrive with increases against flat listenership.
- **An open-exchange in-app sports package:** Cheap, immediately available, and impossible to defend. Nobody could tell them what the placement actually was, and they quietly stopped it without telling anyone it had run.
- **A stadium or team sponsorship enquiry:** Got the rate card, did the maths, closed the spreadsheet. One afternoon cost more than the whole second-half plan.

**7.0 Decision Criteria & Objections**

- **What they weigh:**
    - What exactly is committed, in what unit, and what happens mechanically if it is missed
    - How this clears brand safety in one sentence, before it costs them a meeting
    - Whether the reporting reconciles against something their side will accept
    - Whether the whole thing can be trafficked without their ad operations contractor's Thursday
    - Whether the price sits inside their own signature authority
- **Objections:**
    - "You have no delivery history. Every number you have shown me is a model."
    - "This is a gambling product with a different name on it, and I will not be the one who put us there."
    - "If you underdeliver, make good in what? You will not have the inventory either. That is the same problem twice."
    - "Fifty thousand dollars is my whole test line for the half. If this is wrong I have nothing left to try."

**8.0 Trigger Events (Why Now)**
- The fall plan is being locked, typically June to August, and the sports line is visibly thin
- A podcast or connected TV renewal has landed with a double-digit increase against flat delivery
- A direct competitor announced a sports property or game-day campaign in the last month
- They are new in the role, under six months, and need one line on the plan that is theirs
- An unspent test or innovation budget is approaching a use-it-or-lose-it date

**9.0 Information Diet & Trusted Voices**
- Practitioner newsletters read on the phone before the laptop opens: Marketing Brew, AdExchanger
- Their own delivery reports, which outrank any seller's slide by a distance
- Two or three in-house media peers at non-competing brands, in a group chat or a small community. A peer saying "we ran it, here is what came back" is worth more than every case study ever written
- Ad operations and programmatic communities, for anything about placements, verification and invalid traffic
- LinkedIn, read sceptically, for competitor announcements rather than for opinion

**10.0 Success Metrics (What "Good" Looks Like)**
- From a fall plan any competitor could have bought line for line, to one position inside the game that nobody else on the plan holds
- From modelled reach they cannot defend, to counted exposures reported weekly against a contracted number
- From personally underwriting a publisher's forecast, to a contracted quantity where the shortfall is the publisher's problem
- From "what is new this year" answered with a creator package, to answered with a named position and a delivered report

**11.0 The Message That Lands**
Specific, unglamorous, and answering the brand-safety question before they have to ask it. "InPlay is a free NFL and college football trading competition. Verified fans research all week and trade every snap, all game long, and between fixtures they replay and simulate games in the labs, so the inventory is not bounded by the live game. It is fully simulated, no real money, and we decline betting, daily fantasy, sportsbook and casino outright on every path. Sponsorship starts at $50,000, the exposure quantity is contracted, and if we fall short we extend the flight or deliver it in a following period at no extra cost. Fifteen minutes and I will show you the screen and where the brand sits." Lead with the mechanism and the commitment, kill the gambling question in the first four lines, and never use the word opportunity.

**12.0 Example Titles**
Director of Media · Head of Media · Director of Digital Media · Director of Integrated Media · Senior Media Manager · Director of Customer Acquisition

## Persona 2.2 · Director of Sports Marketing or Brand Partnerships (Champion and Initiator)

**1.0 Archetype Name**
The One Who Goes Looking

**2.0 Role & Functional Identity**

- **2.1 Primary Function:** Owns the brand's relationship with sport: athletes, ambassadors, teams, events and any property that puts the brand into the culture rather than into a feed. The person who is actively hunting for something like InPlay before InPlay calls.
- **2.2 Core Responsibilities:**
    - Sources, negotiates and manages athlete, ambassador and property partnerships
    - Owns the fall and game-day activation calendar and its creative
    - Builds the internal business case for any sports spend and carries it to the media owner and the marketing lead
    - Manages agency, agent and rights-holder relationships
    - Represents the brand at industry and property events, and brings back what is available
    - Reports on the cultural and brand-health side of sports spend, in the absence of hard attribution
- **2.3 Scope, Seniority & Authority:** **Scope** team or function, often one to four people · **Seniority** manager to director · **Decision Authority** can commit small activation budgets alone. For a $50,000 position they are the champion and the architect, not the signer. Their recommendation is what puts it on the media owner's desk.

**3.0 Psychographic Profile**

- **Identity & Self-Image:** The person in the building who actually understands sport, in a marketing team that mostly understands funnels. Proud of the relationships they hold and quietly frustrated that the good ones cost more than the brand can spend.
- **Core Values / What They Optimise For:** Cultural credibility per dollar. Being early to something that later looks obvious. Relationships that survive a budget cut.
- **How They Measure Their Own Worth:** Whether the brand shows up in the moments that matter, whether the properties they brought in got renewed, and whether the marketing lead names their finds in a company meeting.
- **Worldview & Biases Toward This Category:** Professionally obligated to take the meeting, because evaluating things like this is literally the job, and professionally obligated to stress-test it, because their credibility is spent on every recommendation. Believes sport is undervalued internally and is looking for evidence to prove it.

**4.0 A Day in the Life**
The morning is an agent call about an ambassador renewal, and the number has gone up thirty per cent because the athlete's profile grew and the brand's did not. Mid-morning is the activation plan for the season opener: a tailgate, a sweepstake, two social posts and a partnership with a regional bar chain, all of which they have run before. Lunch is with a rights-holder rep who walks through a package that starts at a quarter of a million dollars and does not blink when they say that is the entire annual budget. The afternoon is spent building a one-page case for something small and interesting, knowing the media owner will ask what it delivers and knowing the honest answer is that it will be hard to prove. Late in the day they scroll through what competitors announced this month, find two things the brand cannot afford, and add them to a document nobody else reads.

**5.0 Pains & Fears**

- **5.1 Operational Pains:**
    - **"Everything real in sport starts at a number I do not have."** "The moment a conversation gets interesting, the price arrives and it is the whole year. I have had the same call eleven times."
    - **"I am the only person here who thinks in seasons and everyone else thinks in quarters."** "The season is a fourteen-week window and our planning cycle does not believe in it."
    - **"I cannot prove any of it, so I am always arguing from conviction."** "Ambassadors, activations, game-day. All of it reports in impressions nobody trusts, and I am the one defending it."
- **5.2 Strategic & Political Pains:**
    - **"My budget is the first thing cut and the last thing restored."** "Sport is a nice-to-have on the spreadsheet, whatever anybody says in the brand meeting."
    - **"I bring the ideas and somebody else signs them."** "I am judged on what I source and I control none of the money that sources it."
    - **"When a competitor gets there first, I get asked why we did not."** "Being outbid is treated in this building as being outworked."
- **5.3 The Deepest Fear:** That the function is decorative. That the brand's presence in sport never becomes structural, they spend a career doing tailgates and sweepstakes, and the one property that would have changed it went to a rival who moved faster.

**6.0 What They've Already Tried (and Why It Disappointed)**

- **Athlete and ambassador deals:** Bought credibility but not moments. The athlete posts twice a month while the games, where attention peaks, stay owned by advertisers ten times the brand's size.
- **Game-day activations and tailgates:** Genuinely liked internally, reaches a few thousand people, and produces photographs rather than reach.
- **Regional team or venue sponsorship enquiries:** Priced for national brands even at regional level, and the good inventory is on multi-year deals that will not come up while they are in the job.
- **Fan-engagement and web3 sports platforms:** The previous generation of new sports inventory. Bought access to communities that evaporated and left a specific, lasting scepticism about projected audiences.

**7.0 Decision Criteria & Objections**

- **What they weigh:**
    - Whether the brand gets something ownable and named, rather than a rotation nobody notices
    - Whether the price sits inside a number the media owner can sign without a committee
    - Whether they can describe it in one sentence to a marketing lead who does not follow sport
    - Whether it survives the media owner's questions about measurement, because that is where their cases usually die
- **Objections:**
    - "I have brought in a new sports platform before and it went nowhere. What is different?"
    - "Our media owner will ask what it delivers, and I need an answer before I walk in."
    - "Is this actually ownable, or am I one of nine logos in a rotation?"
    - "If it is trading and football, our compliance and brand people will call it gambling before I finish the sentence."

**8.0 Trigger Events (Why Now)**
- Season planning, when the activation calendar is being written and the good properties are already gone
- An ambassador renewal has come in above budget and freed a conversation about alternatives
- A competitor announced a sports property and leadership asked why the brand does not have one
- A budget review where sport has to justify itself and they need a stronger exhibit
- They have just returned from an industry event with nothing new to show for it

**9.0 Information Diet & Trusted Voices**
- Sports business press, Sportico, Front Office Sports, Sports Business Journal, read daily and personally
- Agents, rights-holder reps and property sellers, trusted for market intelligence and discounted for objectivity
- Peers in the same role at non-competing brands, the single most trusted source on whether something worked
- Industry events and conference floors, where availability is discovered before it is announced
- The competitor announcements they track privately and bring up in every planning meeting

**10.0 Success Metrics (What "Good" Looks Like)**
- From activations that produce photographs, to a named position that runs all season
- From arguing from conviction, to a weekly report they can put in front of the media owner
- From being outbid on everything real, to holding something a bigger rival did not get to first
- From a function treated as decorative, to the person who brought in the thing leadership repeats

**11.0 The Message That Lands**
Treat them as the expert they are and give them the internal ammunition, because they are not the signer. "You already know every football property that matters is on a multi-year deal with somebody ten times your size. InPlay is a free NFL and college football trading competition, and the field itself is for sale: naming rights that run in live games and in every simulation of them, for the whole 142-day season. Fans research all week and trade every snap. It is fully simulated and we decline betting, daily fantasy, sportsbook and casino outright, so the brand-safety conversation is four lines long. Sponsorship starts at $50,000, which is inside your media owner's signature, and the exposure quantity is contracted with a shortfall extended or delivered later at no extra cost. Want the one-pager you can walk into their office with?" Give them the case, not the pitch. This persona sells it internally far better than any seller can.

**12.0 Example Titles**
Director of Sports Marketing · Head of Brand Partnerships · Director of Partnerships · Sports Marketing Manager · Head of Sponsorship · Brand Activation Director

# ICP 3 · The Locked-Out Category Brand

**Dominant pain inherited by both personas below:** HAVING NO ROUTE IN. Their exact customer assembles somewhere they are structurally unable to buy.

**Buying group:** the chief executive or chief marketing officer decides personally and fast. The partnerships or US growth lead builds the case, negotiates the terms and runs it.

---

## Persona 3.1 · Founder, CEO or CMO of a locked-out category brand (Economic Buyer)

**1.0 Archetype Name**
The Insider Without A Door

**2.0 Role & Functional Identity**

- **2.1 Primary Function:** Runs the company or its brand, and personally makes every marketing decision that touches the company's standing. In a category the mainstream keeps at arm's length, that means personally owning every route to market that exists.
- **2.2 Core Responsibilities:**
    - Holds the growth number and answers to a board or investors for it
    - Final sign-off on every marketing and partnership commitment above a modest threshold
    - The public face of the company: press, podcasts, conference stages, a heavily used personal social account
    - Owns the US market plan where the company is expanding into it
    - Negotiates the flagship partnerships personally, because in this category they are the company's most public statements
    - Manages the relationship with counsel, which in a restricted category is a marketing function as much as a legal one
- **2.3 Scope, Seniority & Authority:** **Scope** the whole business · **Seniority** executive, founder or C-suite · **Decision Authority** absolute at this level. Can commit $50,000 to $500,000 in a single conversation with no procurement and no committee, and routinely does.

**3.0 Psychographic Profile**

- **Identity & Self-Image:** A builder operating on hard mode, who believes everything they have is theirs because they built it without the channels everybody else takes for granted. Frequently a participant in this exact audience: trades, follows sport, has an opinion about both.
- **Core Values / What They Optimise For:** Access and legitimacy, meaning channels that treat the brand as premium rather than as a risk. Speed, because the culture signs fast and punishes slow. Being demonstrably first among category rivals.
- **How They Measure Their Own Worth:** US growth, the company's standing relative to its category rivals, and whether the brand appears in places their peers thought were closed to them.
- **Worldview & Biases Toward This Category:** The most receptive buyer InPlay has. Buys early and unproven properties as a deliberate strategy, because waiting for proven has never been available to them. Their scepticism is not "will this work" but "will you still want my category in week six."

**4.0 A Day in the Life**
The day opens with a growth review where the US number is flat and the honest explanation, that the channels which would move it will not accept the category, has been said too many times to say again. Mid-morning is a partnership deck from an agency, and it is another property in another country in front of another audience that is not the one they are trying to reach. Midday they spend forty minutes on a call with counsel about the wording of a campaign that should have shipped a fortnight ago. In the afternoon somebody sends them a competitor's announcement, and they write the message to the board before the board writes to them. In the evening, on their own phone, they are trading, or watching football, or both, and the gap between where they personally spend their attention and where the brand is allowed to appear is the most obvious thing in the world to them and apparently to nobody else.

**5.0 Pains & Fears**

- **5.1 Operational Pains:**
    - **"I have money I am not allowed to spend where my customer is."** "My media plan is a map of exceptions. What is permitted this quarter, who will take us, what got rejected on Tuesday. Strategy is whatever survives the rules."
    - **"Every campaign ships through a legal gauntlet."** "Compliance triples my production time. By the time a campaign clears, the moment it was built for has gone."
    - **"The channels that will take us are the ones nobody wants."** "I am running a premium product through bargain-bin acquisition because the premium doors are shut, and I can see it in the quality of who arrives."
- **5.2 Strategic & Political Pains:**
    - **"My sponsorship portfolio is in the wrong country."** "We pay for properties abroad to build awareness in a market we are not prioritising, because the American ones will not take us. The board sees the invoice and the flat US line on the same page."
    - **"The category leader is pulling away on legitimacy, not on product."** "They get the mainstream moments. Every month without a US flagship we look more like an also-ran, whatever our numbers say."
    - **"I am accountable for a US mandate with no US megaphone."** "Leadership funded the push. If it fails, nobody blames the ad policy. They blame me."
- **5.3 The Deepest Fear:** That the category gets legitimised without them. The doors finally open, but to a rival first, and the brand is permanently filed as second tier in the one market that decides the global winner.

**6.0 What They've Already Tried (and Why It Disappointed)**

- **Sponsorships of properties outside US mainstream sport:** Bought global prestige and a good photograph, and delivered measurably nothing against the US target. The wrong fans in the wrong time zone.
- **Affiliate and influencer saturation:** Delivers volume with fraud and churn, needs legal review on every read, and the best creators already carry a category rival.
- **Platform certification and restricted-format programmes:** Months of process for permission to run a hobbled campaign at a surcharged price that can still be suspended on a Tuesday.
- **A big one-off moment, out of home or a tentpole splurge:** Bought a photograph for the board deck and no durable channel. Presence for a fortnight rather than a position for a season.

**7.0 Decision Criteria & Objections**

- **What they weigh:**
    - Category acceptance, in writing, and whether it survives the publisher's own legal review in month three
    - Whether the audience is genuinely theirs, which they judge personally in about ninety seconds of demonstration
    - Whether it makes the brand look like it belongs somewhere it has not been allowed before
    - Speed. Whether this can be agreed in days, because their whole culture is built that way
- **Objections:**
    - "Everybody takes our money until their lawyers find out. How do I know you will not drop us in week six?"
    - "You have not launched. What am I actually buying today?"
    - "Our last new-platform bet bought a press release and a community that evaporated."
    - "If your own regulatory position wobbles, we are the ones standing next to you in the photograph."

**8.0 Trigger Events (Why Now)**
- A formal US expansion or a new permission, with a funded marketing mandate attached
- A category rival just signed a US sports property and the pressure is immediate and public
- A platform policy shock that just amputated a working channel
- A funding, profitability or token event that created a war chest and an appetite for a statement
- Football season approaching, the one US cultural window where their customer is most reachable, with no compliant way in

**9.0 Information Diet & Trusted Voices**
- Category trade press, read obsessively, plus sports business press where rival deals get announced
- Peer chief executives inside the category, a small world where everybody knows who is buying what
- Their own counsel, who is functionally a co-decision maker. Win the lawyer, win the deal
- Their personal social feed, where they are an active participant rather than a lurker, and where a founder-to-founder message actually gets read
- Category conference circuits, and the mainstream ones they attend specifically to network toward legitimacy

**10.0 Success Metrics (What "Good" Looks Like)**
- From sponsorships in the wrong country, to a season-long position in front of the actual US audience
- From channels that can be switched off overnight, to a contracted position for 142 days
- From a category held at arm's length, to a named surface in an environment their rivals cannot enter
- From affiliate-quality arrivals, to an audience reached in a research and trading context

**11.0 The Message That Lands**
Founder to founder, lead with acceptance and speed, and let the product do the persuading. "Your customer is our audience: verified, financially active, researching all week and trading every snap of every game. You have been locked out of every premium American sports route to that person. We are building one and your category is welcome in it. Field naming rights, sponsored positions inside the trading session, 142 days from 22 August. We are pre-launch, so I am not going to show you an audience projection: the exposure quantity is contracted and if we fall short we extend it or deliver it later at no extra cost. Fifteen minutes on a screen share and you will know in the first two whether this is yours." Keep the first exchange short. This person decides on the demonstration, not the deck, and they will move within days if they want it.

**12.0 Example Titles**
Founder · Chief Executive Officer · Chief Marketing Officer · Chief Growth Officer · President · General Manager, US

## Persona 3.2 · Head of Partnerships or US Growth Lead (Champion and Architect)

**1.0 Archetype Name**
The Workaround Architect

**2.0 Role & Functional Identity**

- **2.1 Primary Function:** Owns the partnerships, sponsorship and alternative-channel portfolio. The person whose entire job exists because the category is blocked, and who builds the acquisition machine out of whatever channels will say yes.
- **2.2 Core Responsibilities:**
    - Sources, negotiates and manages sponsorships, partnerships and direct media deals
    - Runs the affiliate and influencer programmes and argues about their real quality
    - Scouts new inventory continuously and is the first call for anything non-standard
    - Owns channel-level acquisition cost and quality for everything outside the big platforms
    - Prepares the business case and carries new-channel proposals to the chief executive
    - Manages compliance-review logistics for every partner activation
- **2.3 Scope, Seniority & Authority:** **Scope** team or function, a partnerships group of two to fifteen · **Seniority** director, senior director or VP · **Decision Authority** can commit test budgets alone in many organisations. For a season position they are the architect and the champion rather than the signer, but in a blocked category this function carries unusual weight, because it owns the only channels that work.

**3.0 Psychographic Profile**

- **Identity & Self-Image:** The scout and the fixer. Proud of finding channels nobody else knew existed and making them produce. Their career is a portfolio of "I found that one first" stories, and being early to InPlay is exactly the trophy they collect.
- **Core Values / What They Optimise For:** Deal quality, meaning terms, protection and remedies, because they negotiate harder than most sellers they meet. Channel durability, because they have been dropped before. Personal credibility with the chief executive, because every recommendation spends it.
- **How They Measure Their Own Worth:** Channel-level cost against the blended average, the survival rate of their channel picks, and whether the chief executive presents their finds as strategic wins rather than as fixes.
- **Worldview & Biases Toward This Category:** Obligated to take the meeting and obligated to stress-test it harder than anyone. Will ask what sits under every number, will negotiate the remedy line by line, and will call somebody who bought before recommending.

**4.0 A Day in the Life**
The morning is affiliate hygiene: two partners flagged for incentivised traffic, a fortnight of commissions clawed back, and the quality report now needs a caveat paragraph nobody upstairs will read properly. Then three pitch calls, of which two are recycled and one is interesting, and they take all three because once a year one of these is gold and finding it is the job. Midday, legal returns an influencer contract with eleven redlines and the creator's manager will accept four. The afternoon is the channel review, where the chief executive wants a headline channel for the US push and every line on the sheet is saturated, restricted or small. They sketch the channel they wish existed, premium, American, sports, verified, willing, and look at the empty box where the logo should be. When the InPlay email lands they recognise it inside ninety seconds, and then spend two weeks trying to break it before they champion it.

**5.0 Pains & Fears**

- **5.1 Operational Pains:**
    - **"My channel portfolio is held together with duct tape."** "Affiliates commit fraud, influencers go off script, platforms suspend us. I rebuild a quarter of my machine every quarter and call it optimisation."
    - **"I take forty bad meetings to find one real channel."** "Everyone with inventory nobody else wants eventually finds me. My calendar is a junkyard and my job is metal detecting in it."
    - **"Quality reporting is my weekly knife fight."** "Finance sees affiliate volume and thinks we are winning. I see the ninety-day churn and know half of it is worthless. I am the only person paid to tell the truth about my own channels."
- **5.2 Strategic & Political Pains:**
    - **"Every recommendation is my reputation on loan."** "If I champion a channel and it flops, that is remembered. Capital lost on one bad call takes a year of good calls to rebuild."
    - **"Partners drop our category mid-contract."** "I have had platforms take our money and then evolve their policy after their legal review. The clawback is not the wound. The internal embarrassment is."
    - **"If the big doors ever open, my empire shrinks."** "My function exists because we are blocked. I need wins that look strategic rather than compensatory."
- **5.3 The Deepest Fear:** Recommending the big one and being wrong. Pushing the chief executive into a season commitment on a platform that launches to nobody, and becoming the cautionary tale told at their successor's induction.

**6.0 What They've Already Tried (and Why It Disappointed)**

- **Affiliate networks at scale:** The reliable workhorse, but quality decays the moment volume is pushed, and reported cost and real cost diverge embarrassingly.
- **Fan-token, non-fungible-token and metaverse sports platforms:** The previous generation of new sports inventory. Bought access to communities that evaporated, and left a sharpened checklist that InPlay will be measured against line by line.
- **Podcast upfronts:** Worked for years, now renewing at higher prices against flat audiences, with every relevant show already carrying a rival.
- **Brokers' lists of available US properties:** What is available to their category is what nobody else wanted. Minor-league, fringe, or past relevance.

**7.0 Decision Criteria & Objections**

- **What they weigh:**
    - What sits under the delivery commitment: how the quantity was derived and what happens mechanically if it is missed
    - Category commitment in the contract, in language that survives the publisher's own legal review
    - What data they actually receive back, and in what form, because it is ammunition for their internal case
    - Whether the terms are negotiable, because a seller who will not move on remedies is a seller who has not thought about them
- **Objections:**
    - "I have seen this deck before and it was called fan tokens. Show me why your engagement is different from the graveyard."
    - "Pre-launch means I am recommending a forecast. My chief executive signs it and I wear it."
    - "Your makegood is inventory. If you underdeliver you will not have inventory either. Walk me through that."
    - "If your category position wobbles, our legal pulls us out mid-season and I own the wreckage."

**8.0 Trigger Events (Why Now)**
- A core channel just died or degraded, and an open slot needs filling before the season
- The chief executive asked for a headline channel for the US push
- A category rival's sports deal made partnerships the most watched function in the company this month
- Mid-year partnership budget planning with unallocated second-half funds looking for a home
- A warm introduction or launch coverage. This persona responds fastest to being treated as the expert evaluator they are

**9.0 Information Diet & Trusted Voices**
- Affiliate and partnerships communities, where channel war stories travel faster than any press release
- Sponsorship-industry data and deal coverage, used to benchmark whether terms are fair
- Their counterparts in the same role at non-competing blocked brands, the single most trusted source they have
- Performance-marketing analysts who audit channel claims for a living
- Vendor references, always called before a recommendation goes upstairs

**10.0 Success Metrics (What "Good" Looks Like)**
- From a duct-taped portfolio of decaying workarounds, to one durable contracted position they found first
- From defending channel quality every week, to a weekly report that makes the quality case for them
- From compensatory channels, to a property they would defend even if the mainstream doors reopened tomorrow
- From reputation risk on every pitch, to a win the chief executive names in a company meeting

**11.0 The Message That Lands**
Open the books and treat them as the evaluator they are, because they will stress-test this whether you invite it or not. "You evaluate new inventory for a living, so here is the real picture. InPlay is a free NFL and college football trading competition launching 22 August for 142 days. Verified account holders, KYC at account level, age and geography gated. They research all week and trade every snap, and the labs mean they replay and simulate games between fixtures, so the inventory is not bounded by the live game. We have not launched, so I am not going to show you an audience number. What I will show you is the mechanism, the position, and the commitment: a contracted exposure quantity, and if we fall short you choose between an extended flight and delivery in a following period, at no additional cost. Break it if you can. If you cannot, you are the one who found this in August rather than hearing about it in February." Never oversell. This persona champions what survives their own stress test, and then sells it upstairs better than any seller could.

**12.0 Example Titles**
Head of Partnerships · Director of Partnerships · VP Business Development · Head of US Growth · Director of Sponsorships · Head of Affiliate and Influencer Marketing
