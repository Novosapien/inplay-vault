# Discovery / Home -- Changelog

| Date | Change | Source |
|------|--------|--------|
| 2026-05-09 | Initial placeholder created from component 1 deep-dive extraction | [[08-05-2026-component-1-simulation-app]] |
| 2026-08-10 | **Home restructured, and a first-open tour added ahead of it.** New top-level tabs — **Teams / League / Schedule / Games** — with search at the top, a favourites rail, a live-game rail, top movers, and division filtering across NFL and NCAA. "Schedule is forward-looking, games is backward-looking" (George). The **League** tab is new: standings and leaders. Ahead of all of it sits Edwin's **four-card explainer** and the **fork screen** (email-only vs Persona-verified) — see [[customer-onboarding/customer-onboarding]] — which he called the one non-negotiable: today a referred user's first screen is a stadium image and a referral bank, which explains nothing. | [[07-08-2026-touchdown]] |
| 2026-08-10 | **Ticker to become clickable and configurable.** Cody's "hamster wheel" intent: click a team in the ticker, land on its team page, trade it there, click onward. Two problems with the current one — it is **not clickable**, and at 32 NFL teams plus college on a mixed week you would wait ~15 minutes for your team to come round again. Proposed: **cap it at ~20 entries**, composed from **top movers + favourites + live games**, with a **"configure my ticker" setting**. George's caveat: it is a small moving target, so clickability needs testing before it is assumed. | [[07-08-2026-touchdown]] |
| 2026-08-13 | **The ticker should carry IPO prices rather than nothing.** With no secondary prices until each league's market opens, the app currently suppresses the ticker rather than show blank or zero values, which George argued protects the user from meaningless data. Edwin's alternative, agreed as better: **show the IPO price with a small "IPO" label**, so the ticker scrolls with real numbers from the moment someone opens the app. His reasoning is about enticement rather than information: a first-time user should see something moving and priced, because a static wall gives them no reason to stay. Note his related design intent, that the home page deliberately shows no team scores, so the ticker reads as a market rather than a scoreboard. | [[12-08-2026-touchdown]] |
| 2026-08-17 | **Surface reduction, requested five days before the offering.** Edwin's direction is to cut rather than add, on the grounds that too many clickable surfaces confuse a first-time user. The shape he asked for: **money first** on the front page, then a **live games** section (renaming Gamecast to "live", with the tiles taking you into the Gamecast where trading is available), **news behind an icon** in the top bar rather than occupying the page, and **rankings**. He questioned whether a discover page needs to exist at all given the search control at the top. The goal in his words: _"make this UI light, make it effective, simple"_, with surfaces thin enough that a user does not scroll deep to reach anything. Also: **remove the floating orange trade button everywhere**, superseded by the persistent buy and sell of the one-click model. Jared agreed and added that there is _"still a good amount of fat we can cut"_ that detracts from a user's understanding. **Worth recording why the fat exists:** Edwin was fair about it on the call, noting the app was deliberately built with many surfaces because the plan assumed advertising inventory needing places to live. With no advertisers signed, that rationale has gone, and the surfaces became cost without benefit. | [[17-08-2026-touchdown]] |
