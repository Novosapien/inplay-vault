| 2026-08-17 | **The strategy lab is now the business case, not a feature.** Edwin's position on 14-08, stated plainly: _"we don't have any advertisers. So the only way we can try to make money at this point is through the subscription."_ That makes this sub-component the revenue product rather than a retention feature, and he confirmed it is the number one priority once the trading experience is fixed. **What he demonstrated, with real numbers:** a rule typed in plain language ("sell after every home touchdown, cover eight plays later"), run against one team, returned net $6,854 across 20 trades at a 75% win rate with a small maximum drawdown, about $7,000 better than buy-and-hold. Run across all 138 teams it returned roughly **$1.1m and was profitable on 129 of them**. The parameter sweep is the actual product: changing "eight plays" to four dropped it to $93,000 and to twelve raised it to $1.3m. Every triggering incident is listed and clickable through to a replay, so a user can inspect what else was happening around a winning signal. Seventeen sample strategies ship built-in, and users save their own. **His commercial argument is that it has value outside InPlay:** a prediction-market trader can use it today, which widens the addressable buyer beyond people who trade the challenge. **The ask that gates it:** multi-year Sportradar history, four to five seasons rather than one, so a strategy can be tested across more than a single year. Note the tension with [[compliance/compliance]] to resolve before it is sold: charging for something that materially improves a paying user's results is the thing that moves the classification, and this is squarely that. | [[14-08-2026-touchdown]] |
