> acceptance AC1). Stream B of the two-stream fix-set build.

## What we did

1. **Instrumented the tick** (`mm/runtime/loop.py`) — per-stage timers
   for readings drain · venue drain · sweep · converge · commit · ack
   flush · checkpoint · publish, plus the whole tick and the whole loop
   pass. Reports ms-per-ack, ms-per-cycle, ms-per-reading, per-stage
   p50/p90/p99, AC2's p90 acks-per-tick and AC4's miss ratio with its
   denominator.
2. **Built THE SIX-GAME WORKLOAD** (`scripts/six_game_workload.py`) —
   the fixed baseline every later performance AC measures against. Six
   copies of the Chiefs–Ravens capture under fresh synthetic ids,
   Z-stamped, twelve distinct securities, seeded stagger, the
   publisher's 2 s re-offer contract, the full 170-book universe.
3. **Ran the profile on a prod-spec clone** — three arms: 10× timers-on
   (900 s), 10× timers-off (900 s, the overhead pair), 1× timers-on
   (2,400 s, the tick-budget truth).
4. **Wrote the profile doc** with the drain-cap ruling.

916 tests green (885 baseline + 31 new), ruff clean, mypy clean, on both
the Mac and the clone. Re-verified after the resumption.

**The clone is STOPPED, not deleted** (`cb1-profile-clone`, us-east4-a,
n2-standard-2) so CB4 can restart it and skip the ~20-minute rebuild —
including for the `--games 3` arm below. It carried **only the project
default compute service account; no production service account was ever

## What we learned

### ⭐ The venue ack drain is 98% of tick time

The headline, and it overturns the working assumption. At **1× — the
tick-budget truth per R1** — over 1,747 ticks with all six games live:

| Stage | p50 | p99 | share of tick time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **venue (ack drain)** | **1,162 ms** | **2,449 ms** | **98.1%** |
| sweep (170 cycles) | 24 ms | 29 ms | 1.8% |
| readings | 0.31 ms | 5.2 ms | 0.08% |
| commit (the N31 fsync) | 2.4 ms | 3.5 ms | — |

(The 10× arm agrees on the shape: venue 94.4%, sweep 5.2%.)

Everything the fix-set was planning to optimise is in the **1.8%**. The
sweep — 170 books, every 500 ms — costs **0.143 ms per cycle** and is
remarkably stable (p50 0.143 vs p99 0.172). It is not the problem.

**The tick is 1.19 s against a 500 ms interval, and 63% of ticks ran
late.** The loop is in permanent backlog under this workload.

### ⭐ ms-per-ack is superlinear in book depth, not a constant

Same code, same machine, same workload — the per-ack cost more than
quadrupled as the games came live and the resting book deepened:

| Window | Mean acks/tick | ms per ack (p50) |
|---|---|---|
| 60 s shakedown, games still staggering in | shallow | **1.455** |
| 900 s full run at 10×, all six live | 48.8 | **6.313** |
| **2,400 s full run at 1×**, all six live | **129.1** | **9.893** |

Per-ack cost tracks **batch depth, not game speed**: 2.6× the acks per
tick buys 1.57× the cost of each one. This matters beyond CB1: **every
capacity projection that multiplies a constant ms-per-ack by an event
rate is optimistic**, and the NCAA-Saturday numbers in the vault are
exactly that shape.

A counter-intuitive detail worth carrying forward: **the 1× arm is the
more loaded of the two.** The ack stream is the engine's own
instructions coming back from the venue, generated by the 170-book
converger at a rate set by the wall clock — not by capture playback
speed. So a 2,400 s arm sits with a deep resting book far longer than a
900 s one. Do not read "1×" as "light".

### 🔴 The profile fails R1's own sanity check, and that is recorded, not hidden

R1 says a profile wildly off the 13/14-08 evidence is itself suspect.

⚠ **Correcting how that bar gets quoted, because it changes the
verdict.** R1 and the CB1 brief both render the evidence as "~0.5–1
ms/event, **~35% late ticks**". **The vault never says late ticks.**
Every primary source says **missed sweeps on ~35% of ticks** under three
live games — `decisions.md` twice (the post-window-fix residual, and
phase B's honest scope), `market-maker.md`'s standing faults, and the
08-14 dead-man session note. The ~35% is a **MISS RATIO**. Compared
correctly:

| | 13/14-08 evidence (3 games) | 6 games 1× | **3 games 1× (validation)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| per-event cost | 0.5–1 ms *(never measured)* | 9.893 ms/ack | **8.502 ms/ack** |
| missed sweeps | **~35% of ticks** | 50.1% of all ticks | **22.1% of all ticks** (23.5% of due-sweep) ✅ |
| late ticks | *no live counterpart* | 63.1% | 51.0% |

**At production's own game count the miss ratio brackets the live
evidence from the right side** — 23.5% against ~35%, same books, same
games. The rig is slightly *less* stressed than production, which is
what §3 predicts (no wire, no fills, fewer readings). The sanity check
passes on every term that was ever measured.

**And CB1 confirms the vault's own diagnosis rather than upsetting it.**
`decisions.md` already names the cause of the 35% missed sweeps as
**per-event engine cost**, and sets the fix chain as "phase B → *the
per-event cost measurement* → optimization". **CB1 is that
measurement**, and it says the per-event cost is the venue ack drain at
98.1% of the tick. The two agree on mechanism.

### ✅ The validation arm RESOLVED the per-ack gap — by refuting my first answer

The lead authorized a `--games 3 --speed 1` arm (2,100 s, 20:53→21:28Z)
to settle it. It did, and **my reconciliation was wrong.**

I predicted three games would be a shallower book and per-ack cost would
fall toward the 0.5–1 ms bar. **It did not** — 9.893 → **8.502 ms/ack**,
a 14% drop for half the games. ⭐ **Because the ack stream is not driven
by games.** Only 12 of 170 books carry one; the other 158 are swept and
converged regardless. Total acks fell just 12% (225,596 → 197,730).
**Book count drives the ack volume, not game count** — the more useful
fact, and the one CB4 should carry.

**The real resolution: the two numbers never measured the same thing,
and only one of them was ever measured at all.**

| Source | Figure | What it actually is |
|---|---|---|
| `decisions.md` 04-08 | **0.17 ms/event** | Measured — but its own caveat says it *"excludes the sync driver's diff, payload serialisation, the NATS publish, **inbound ack processing**, and the sweep over all 170 — it is the engine core only"* |
| `drafts/always-quoting-step4-design.md` §4 | **~0.5–1 ms/ack** | An **ESTIMATE** in a throughput argument. Never measured. |
| **CB1** | **8.5–9.9 ms/ack** | Measured, **including everything the 0.17 figure excluded** — and inbound ack processing above all |

**So CB1 contradicts no measurement — it contradicts an unmeasured
estimate, which is exactly what R1 sent it to test.** The one real prior
measurement carved out the ack path; the ack path is where the tick
goes. The two results are consistent.

The step4 design asked for precisely this run — *"the already owed
measurement … tells us whether engine-cost optimization is the real
Saturday gap"*. **It is, and worse than it feared:** its own arithmetic
said ~2,100 instructions/s would cost "~1–2 s of every second on acks";
at the measured rate that is **~17.9 s of compute per second.** The
conclusion was right and understated by ~an order of magnitude.

🔴 **Carry forward:** the ~0.5–1 ms/ack estimate still sits in the step4
design and anything built on it. **Those projections are optimistic by
10–18× and need re-deriving from this profile.**

That is an explanation, not a proof. The cheap test that settles it is a
`--games 3 --speed 1` arm (~35 min; staggers 240/480/1500 s need ~2,100
s). It was **not run** — the run plan was closed and the clone was due to
stop. **CB4 should run it before optimising**, because it also tells CB4
whether it is chasing a constant or a slope. Until then the *absolute*
ms figures are provisional; the *relative* findings (drain ≫ sweep,
superlinear cost, the cap direction) hold at every point measured.

### ⭐ The drain-cap re-size we have been carrying as owed is BACKWARDS

`[drain-cap]` in `loop.py` and the build-deploy-log's step-1 row both
say the venue cap must RISE toward ~1,050 acks/tick since group commit,
because "the binding constraint becomes engine time". The first half is
confirmed — commit is 2.4 ms against a 1,162 ms drain. The conclusion is
inverted by the same fact. At the measured **9.893 ms/ack** (1×):

- cap 512 (today) → worst-case drain **5.1 s**, and the measured max
  venue stage is already 2.5 s;
- cap 1,050 → **10.4 s**, 2× past the 5 s beat-stall threshold, so the
  heartbeat would be WITHHELD and the dead-man would sweep the book.

Raising the cap turns a bounded, alarmed deferral into a
heartbeat-starving tick — the exact failure the cap exists to prevent,
and the same shape as the 08-13 dead-man fire loop. **CB1 proposes NO
dictionary change.** The cap is not the lever; the per-ack cost is. The
relationship for later: `venue cap ≤ drain budget ÷ ms-per-ack`; a
1,050 cap needs ms-per-ack ≤ **0.286 ms, a 35× improvement.**

The cap also barely fires: **4 ticks in 1,747 at 1× (0.23%)**, all
venue. It is not what is costing us.

Incidentally the readings cap (256) has 23–43× headroom — readings
peaked at 6/tick at 1× and 11/tick at 10× — and never fired in either
arm. Nobody should spend time on it.

### ⚠ AC4 is not reachable by CB2 and CB3 alone

The AC4 target is a miss ratio < 0.5%. The measured baseline is **52.3%
at 1×** (876 missed of 1,676 due-sweep ticks) and 8.97% at 10×. **That
is a >100× improvement required at 1×.** CB2 (de-phasing) and CB3 (the
incremental sweep) both act on the sweep side — **1.8%** of tick time.
Even a *perfect* incremental sweep saves ~24 ms of a 1,187 ms tick. The
misses are caused by the venue drain overrunning the slot, so **the
chunk that can move AC4 is CB4**, which the spec currently timeboxes to
one session and scopes as "only where the profile says it pays". The
profile says it pays there and almost nowhere else. → for George.

CB2 is the partial exception and should keep its slot: because per-ack
cost is superlinear in batch depth, flattening the ack clump cuts the
cost of each ack too, so it is the one sweep-side chunk that also
attacks the drain.

**AC2's baseline (1×, the harsher and authoritative one): p90 acks-per-
tick = 222**, so CB2 must reach ≤ 111. At 10× it is 116 → ≤ 58.

### The prod VM is not an e2-medium

R1 specifies a clone of "e2-medium, 2 vCPU". Read-only describe (no SSH):
`inplay-market-maker` is **n2-standard-2**, Intel Cascade Lake, Debian 12,
us-east4-a. e2-medium is 2 *shared* vCPU with burst credits; profiling
there would have run slower than production and drifted as credits
drained, which is R1's own "a Mac profile lies" failure in a different
costume. The clone matches the real spec; the deviation is recorded in
the profile doc and flagged to the lead.

### ⭐ AC2's baseline was confounded and had to be re-cut (review HIGH)

The #33 review caught that CB1's first AC2 baseline — p90 acks-per-TICK
— **cannot gate CB2**:

> acks per tick = ack arrival rate × **pass duration**

Both arms reproduce the identity exactly (93.9/s × 1.375 s = 129;
77.9 × 0.626 = 48.8). So the metric **rises when the loop gets slower**,
the opposite of what the gate should reward, and a within-pulse offset
cannot shift a per-pass count when a 1× pass spans 2.75 pulses. R2/AC2
are amended to **p90 of acks ARRIVING per fixed 500 ms wall-clock
window**; `scripts/ac2_ack_windows.py` computes it.

**No re-run was needed** — the journals already carry each ack's arrival
at microsecond precision in `receive_time` (the venue's own enqueue
stamp). Not `accepted_time`, which is when the drain reached it and
would re-import the same confound.

**New baseline: p90 = 214** per 500 ms window at 1× six games (gate
≤ 107); 144 at three games; 88 at 10×.

The arrival shape is the finding underneath it: **the median 500 ms
window is EMPTY and 66% of windows contain no acks at all.** These are
not clumps but spikes — p90 holds near 220 from a 500 ms bucket down to
a 20 ms one, inter-arrival gaps inside a burst run **0.018 ms** at p50,
and there are **1,641 gaps > 100 ms against 1,747 passes**. One burst
per pass, near enough exactly.

📝 **What that means for CB2, and it changes how to judge it:**
de-phasing will **not spread a burst** — the loop publishes once per
pass whatever the offsets are. What it changes is **how many books are
in each burst**: with 8 buckets a 1× pass crosses ~2.75 of them, so ~34%
of books. 🟡 Projected p90 ≈ 73 against the ≤ 107 gate, so AC2 looks
reachable — **judge burst SIZE, not burst spacing.**

⚠ And the caveat that must travel with the number: the synthetic venue
answers *inside* the publish call with no wire, so this is the tightest
the spike can physically be. Production's gateway smears it. The
baseline is sound as a **relative** gate on the same rig; the
66%-empty figure is **not** a fact about the live system.

### Instrumentation overhead is 4.24 µs/tick — AC1 passed

Measured directly on the clone (100,000 iterations of a full tick's
timer sequence: `start()` + 14 `mark()` + `close_tick()`, best of 7):
**timers ON 4.925 µs, OFF 0.685 µs, added 4.240 µs.** That is 0.00085%
of the 500 ms interval and **0.094% even of the 4.5 ms quiet tick** —
four orders of magnitude inside AC1's 5%.

The whole-workload A/B was *noise-dominated* and could never have been
otherwise: deltas within ±4.33% with **inconsistent sign** (turning
timers OFF made the engine slower on three of five metrics, which is
impossible as a real effect), against a signal ~5 orders of magnitude
smaller than the variance. The micro-measurement is the number to quote;
the A/B is a consistency check, not a measurement. Saying which of two
measurements to trust is more useful than averaging them.

Why it is this small by construction: with timers off the runtime holds
`NO_TIMERS`, whose `mark()` returns the caller's own mark and calls no
clock at all. The two modes differ only in whether `perf_counter` runs.

## What went wrong / got stuck

- **Two streams were editing ONE working tree.** stream-a-ca1 switched
  the shared `inplay-market-maker` checkout to their branch while my
  410 lines of instrumentation sat uncommitted in `loop.py`. Caught
  because a profile run printed CA1's anchor-seed log line. Fixed
  without losing anyone's work: verified `loop.py` was 100% mine, saved
  a patch, made a git worktree, and reverted my hunks in the shared tree
  with `git apply -R` — deliberately NOT `git checkout --`, which would
  have destroyed CA1's uncommitted files. 📝 **Lesson for the runbook:
  parallel streams get one worktree each.** Phase 2 has CA2 and CB2 both
  down to touch `venue/sync.py`; in one checkout that is not
  "coordinate the diff", it is two agents overwriting a file.
- **`uv sync` installed Python 3.14 on the clone.** Profiling on 3.14
  when the repo targets 3.12 would have been the same class of error as
  profiling on a Mac. Pinned to 3.12.13. ⚠ The live VM's exact patch
  version is still unverified — reading it needs an SSH to the live box,
  which R1 forbids. One line for whoever is next on that VM legitimately.
- **`inplay-vpc` has no SSH ingress rule at all**, so IAP could not
  reach a clone placed there. Rather than add a firewall rule to a
  production VPC for a measurement, the clone went on the `default`
  network, which already allows SSH. The workload needs no network, so
  nothing was lost.
- **macOS `tar` smuggled AppleDouble `._*` files into the upload** and
  `test_config.py`'s marker scan died decoding one. Not a code fault —
  re-tarred with `COPYFILE_DISABLE=1`. Worth knowing before anyone else
  ships a tree to a VM this way.
- **The worker process died mid-chunk and a second worker finished it.**
  The first CB1 worker timed out at ~19:13Z, ~11 minutes after the last
  run arm completed at 19:02Z, with the code committed but unpushed, the
  profile doc holding four unfilled placeholders, and this note written
  off the 10× arm only. 📝 **The recovery worked because the runs were
  launched under `nohup` behind a `run_profile.sh` that wrote a
  `profile.done` sentinel** — so the second worker could prove the three
  arms had finished rather than guess, and re-ran nothing. That is the
  08-14 lesson (*"an evidence capture owed at a deadline needs its own
  timer"*) paying off in the same day it was written. **Runbook rule:
  any measurement run longer than a few minutes goes under `nohup` with
  a completion sentinel, never on a live SSH stream.**
- **Both completion watchers on the validation arm failed silently, and
  the lead had to chase the result.** The arm finished cleanly at
  21:28:48Z (rc=0); I did not report until the lead asked at ~22:00Z.
  One watcher was a backgrounded poll loop — killed at its 10-minute
  tool timeout, ~25 minutes before the run ended. The second was a
  persistent monitor armed precisely because I distrusted the first, and
  it never fired either. 📝 **The lesson is not "arm two watchers" — I
  did, and got nothing. It is that a watcher's own liveness must be
  checked on a schedule the work owns.** The run itself was never at
  risk: `nohup` plus the `validation.done` sentinel meant the result sat
  safely on disk the whole time. **The sentinel is what worked twice
  today; the notification path is what failed twice.** Poll the
  sentinel on a cadence rather than trusting an event.
- **A doc written before its last run arm lands is a trap.** The
  placeholders were caught, but the *prose* around them had already been
  written off the 10× numbers and read as finished — 94% not 98%, 6.31
  not 9.893 ms/ack, 8.97% not 52.3% miss ratio, and the R1 sanity-check
  paragraph asserted a comparison whose number did not exist yet. Every
  headline in the doc and this note had to be re-derived. 📝 **Write the
  analysis after the data, or mark every provisional figure as loudly as
  the placeholders.**
