# 2026-08-14 — CA3: the ask cap, resize not reject (F3 / R-Q08)

## What we did

Built R-Q08: the ask ladder may not offer more than we can legally sell,
and when it would, the ladder is **RESIZED** rather than the book
rejected.

The rule underneath is R-V07, discovered by live probe on 08-09 and
recorded nowhere in any vendor document: a side-2 sell may not exceed
`Pos − livS`, and over that line tZERO refuses the **whole order** — it
never part-fills and never opens a short. An unbounded ladder therefore
does not lose one rung when it breaches; it loses the entire ask side of
that book.

Four pieces:

1. `src/mm/position/sellable.py` (new). The read seam: turns the position
   engine's Net Position and the Venue State Record's open orders into
   one integer, `capacity = holding − livS`.
2. `src/mm/quotes/engine.py`. `cap_ask_ladder()` — the resize — plus the
   `AskCap` record on `Published` and the edge-triggered alarm.
3. `src/mm/venue/engine.py`. `pending_quantity` on `VenueOrder`, so an
   in-flight replace can be counted at max(old, new).
4. `src/mm/orchestration/engine.py`. Four lines: assemble the bound, pass
   it to the cycle.

## What we learned

### 1 · livS must EXCLUDE the resting states, or the book oscillates

The requirement says `Σ(asks) ≤ holding − livS` with livS as "acknowledged
resting asks + in-flight ask quantity". Implemented literally, that
double-counts the ask side against itself, and the failure is not subtle.

The reconciler's ladder is a **replacement** for our resting asks, not an
addition to them: a still-wanted price keeps its order (rest-until-gone,
N10), a moved price replaces it, an unwanted price is cancelled. So a
fully-offered book computes a bound of zero, empties its ask side, watches
the cancels drop livS back to zero, re-offers the full ladder, and
oscillates — at the 500 ms LIVE pulse, across every book.

The correct question is **"can the next reconciler pass reclaim this
quantity?"**, and the reconciler answers it itself: `_ACTIONABLE` is
exactly `{ACTIVE, PARTIALLY_FILLED}`. Everything else is untouchable this
round. So livS counts `PENDING_SUBMIT`, `PENDING_REPLACE`,
`PENDING_CANCEL` and `UNKNOWN`, and the resting states are excluded
because the ladder already accounts for them.

This is the CA2 lesson in a new place: **§4.4's `_EXPOSURE_STATES` is not
a general-purpose set.** It answers "could this cost us money". livS
answers "what has the venue committed that this pass cannot take back".
A test now pins the two sets apart so a future edit to §4.4 cannot drag
livS along with it.

### 2 · The E27 stub of 0 is UNKNOWN, not a holding of nothing

This is the finding that decides whether the chunk is safe to deploy, and
it is George's to rule on.

R-V07's `Pos` is the **venue's** position, not our journal's. Our journal
starts at net 0 on every fresh journal, and the two are known to differ:
on 14-08 the engine sold IPTCJETS to a net of −197 from a zero journal
position, and the venue accepted every sell, because the ACCOUNT holds
seeded inventory the journal never saw.

Wired naively with `opening_position_shares` at its 0 default, the bound
is `0 − livS`, which is never positive. **Every book goes bid-only on the
first tick after a cutover.** We proved it rather than argued it: wiring
the bound unconditionally turned three existing tests red, all of them on
a missing ask side, and the rig drill's own never-empty check would have
followed.

So the bound fails OPEN when there is no published opening position, and
says so once at boot (`ASK_CAP_UNBOUNDED`). That is the direction the
codebase already chose for R-Q09 — "refusing on absent data would silence
quoting, and a market maker that stops quoting has failed at its job"
(decisions 2026-08-14f) — and an unpublished IPO allocation is absent
data of exactly that kind. It is E27's gap, not evidence about what the
account holds.

The whole mechanism turns on with **one number** the day E27 closes or an
operator supplies the account's real holding. Nothing else changes.

### 3 · AC9 holds by construction, and the reason is worth keeping

The bound is built partly from venue **intent** — pending submits and
replaces registered in memory before the venue answers — and replay never
re-drives `converge()`. A replayed run therefore computes a *different*
bound.

It does not matter, because of where the cap lands. It is applied after
the §5.8 materiality decision, after every §5.7.3 seeded draw and after
the reshape, on the final quantities only. `_BookRecord` holds prices,
**pre-variation** quantities and the IA, so a capped size changes no field
of `state()`; the version chain is untouched; the alarm flag lives outside
the checkpoint. A test drives the live arm with an 8,000-share in-flight
sell the replay arm never sees, and both reach byte-identical core state.

⚠ The converse is the standing constraint, recorded in the code: **if a
future change ever puts Target Order Book quantities into checkpointed
state, this argument dies** and the cap has to move to the edge.

### 4 · The drop threshold is the MINIMUM, not the increment

The brief said to drop a capped level under 500 shares (the §5.7.3
increment). It has to be 1,000 (the §5.7.3 **minimum**): §5.10's
`quantities_within_bounds` rejects any level below the minimum, and a
failed check raises and blocks the **whole book** — a reject, the one
outcome R-Q08 exists to prevent. The increment still binds: a capped
quantity is FLOORED to the grid, never rounded, because rounding up would
breach the bound the whole mechanism exists to respect.

The grid is a **parameter** defaulting to the dictionary row, not a pinned
500 — which matters, because George's 15-08 ruling takes `qty_increment`
500 → 1 (MM PR #36, raw share counts). The cap follows that row instead of
fighting it, and a test pins the arithmetic at both grids.

### 5 · The cap bounds the TARGET — and that was a DEFECT, not a limitation

⚠ **This finding was recorded wrong first, and the correction is the
lesson.** The original text called it a named limitation and argued no
venue rule breaks, because R-V07 is applied per order at SUBMIT time and
each standing order was legal when sent. review-002 falsified that with a
probe, and the reasoning failed in two places at once.

What the probe measured, at reference price 63.7825 against an 18,000
share bound: a kept rung of 12,500 shares plus a replace of 14,500
settled at **27,000 shares against an 18,000 share holding**. The next
ask would be whole-order rejected under R-V07 — the exact outcome R-Q08
exists to prevent. And the claim that the cap "declines to ADD" was
simply untrue: a pass-2 replace adopts the new rank's drawn size and can
RAISE a live order's commitment.

Two mistakes, both worth naming:

1. **A per-order rule was read as a per-order property.** Each standing
   order was legal when sent. That says nothing about whether the NEXT
   one can be, which is what the bound is for.
2. **The test pinned the behaviour instead of the requirement.** It
   asserted what the code did — no cancels, no submits — and called the
   result a documented limitation. A test that ratifies the current
   behaviour cannot fail when that behaviour is wrong.

The fix keeps the resize at mint time and gives it the input it was
missing. `resting_ask_quantities` collects price → resting ask shares
over the reconciler's `_ACTIONABLE` states; orchestration passes it
beside the bound; the cap RESERVES the kept rungs before it spends
anything on the levels that will actually be sent. A kept level keeps its
drawn quantity so its price stays in the target — dropping it would make
the reconciler CANCEL a still-wanted rung, which is Edwin's N10.

⚠ **Residual, open:** if the kept rungs ALONE exceed the bound, no target
can fix it. Only a cancel brings that down, and cancelling a still-wanted
rung is N10's to revisit. It cannot bite while the cap is dark (E27), and
it must be closed before N43 activation. → team lead's Drift Log, folded
into the N43 package.

- The rig drill at 120× fails its "final book two-sided" check. It fails
  on the **base commit too, and worse** (two one-sided books there, one
  here, on opposite sides), so it is a pre-existing smoke-run artefact of
  the accelerated pacing at the final whistle, not a CA3 regression. Both
  arms pass the two replay-equality checks.
- **The worker died mid-drill and nearly took finished work with it.** It
  wrote the finding-5 tests AFTER it pushed, then died on an API timeout
  before it committed them. The tests survived only as uncommitted files
  in the worktree. Recovered and pushed as 62c5d96. The lesson is small
  and cheap: commit the test, then run the long job.
- ⚠ **The `MM_DRILL_DIR` trap.** `scripts/a2_replay_drill.py` deletes its
  scratch directory at start (`shutil.rmtree`). The default is
  `scripts/a2-run/`, and those files are TRACKED IN GIT. A
  default-directory run destroys committed fixtures before it writes its
  own. Always set `MM_DRILL_DIR` to a path outside the repo. The warning
  now lives in the drill's own **docstring**, not only in this note,
  because the docstring is what the next runner actually reads. A note
  warns the person who already knows to look.
- ⚠ **The drill's stdout is BLOCK-BUFFERED into its log file.** The log
  lags by kilobytes, so a frozen `tail` looks exactly like a stall and
  invites a needless restart of a multi-hour run. Judge a drill's
  liveness by **journal growth**, never by the log. The 08-15 monitor
  heartbeats on `journal.jsonl` line count, and alarms separately if the
  process disappears without writing its sentinel.
- ⚠ **`MARKETABLE_GUARD_BLIND` fires on the loopback rig**: the guard is
  ON but has never held a book (0 book messages), so R-Q09 refuses
  NOTHING on a rig drill. The rig gateway publishes no `market.book`.
  A rig drill therefore proves nothing about R-Q09 — recorded as a GATE
  prerequisite, which needs the rig configured for book publishing or
  synthetic and live evidence instead. The pleasant half: the alarm is
  the CA2 MED-2 observability fix working on its first real outing.

## Decisions made

- livS excludes `ACTIVE` / `PARTIALLY_FILLED`; it is derived from the
  reconciler's reach, not inherited from §4.4. → decisions.md
- A 0 opening position means UNKNOWN, so the bound fails open and
  announces itself. → decisions.md, and George's to overturn.
- The capped-level drop threshold is `min_quantity`, not `qty_increment`.
- The opening position joins the arithmetic **at the cap**, never through
  `PositionEngine`, so it can never move the Position Ratio, the
  Inventory Adjustment or any price.

### 6 · A FIX total is not a remainder (review MED-1, 08-15)

The Phase-3 review found the one real bug in this chunk, and it was an
arithmetic scale error rather than a design fault.

`pending_quantity` is a FIX **total**: `ReplaceOrder.quantity` is "CumQty
+ the rank's draw", because the gateway refuses a replace quantity at or
below CumQty. `leaves_qty` is what **remains**. livS took `max()` of the
two directly, so a partially-filled sell being repriced counted its
filled shares twice — once in livS, and once through the journalled net
position those same fills had already reduced.

The repro: a 10,000-share sell with 6,000 filled, being repriced,
contributed 10,000 to livS instead of 4,000. Against a 14,000 holding the
bound read 4,000 where it should read 10,000.

⚠ Note WHICH books it hit. A book only reaches this state by trading —
partial fill, then a reprice. The bound was therefore at its most wrong
on exactly the books a working market maker has, and correct on the idle
ones a test tends to reach for. That is the shape of bug a drill at rest
never finds.

The fix counts the destination at `pending_quantity − cum_qty`, so both
operands are remaining shares. The `None` fallback for an old checkpoint
then sits on that same scale, instead of comparing a total against a
remainder.

Also from the same review: the lenient asks `zip` is now scoped to the
capped path (`strict=cap is None`) — a dropped level is the only
legitimate reason for a short quantity list, and on the uncapped path a
mismatch should raise rather than truncate a book in silence.

Both fixes were pinned by tests **proved to fail without them** — each
fix was reverted in turn to confirm the test caught it. A test written
against a bug you have already fixed is worth very little until you have
watched it fail.

### 7 · N45 turned two questions into three (08-15, merge train)

PR #41 (N45) deleted `_EXPOSURE_STATES` and replaced it with
`_REPLAYABLE_EXPOSURE_STATES`, which answers a **narrower** question:
"what can a journal replay rebuild". It excludes `PENDING_SUBMIT`,
because `register_submit` runs before the venue answers and nothing
journals that call.

That broke this chunk twice over, and the second break is the
interesting one:

1. The import died, which is loud and cheap.
2. `test_livs_is_a_subset_of_the_exposure_set` had its **premise
   inverted**, not its name changed. livS counts `PENDING_SUBMIT`; the
   new venue set does not; so the subset claim became materially false
   while still compiling.

There are now three questions, and conflating any two is a bug:

| Set | Question |
|---|---|
| §4.4 as written | Could this order cost us money? |
| `_REPLAYABLE_EXPOSURE_STATES` | Can a journal replay rebuild it? |
| livS | What has the venue committed that this pass cannot reclaim? |

⚠ **livS counts `PENDING_SUBMIT` and the replayable set does not, and
that divergence is deliberate.** Those shares are committed at the venue
whatever a replay can reconstruct. A bound that ignored them would offer
the same shares twice.

The tests now define §4.4's list locally instead of importing it —
importing would silently re-point the assertions the next time the venue
set moves — and the divergence is pinned through the **public**
`pending_exposure()` reading, so a rename cannot break the file again.
