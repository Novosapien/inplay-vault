## Why this document exists

Edwin sent a second, complete market maker on 30-07. It is not a note on ours;
it is a different machine. He initially believed it could be plugged into tZERO
and run. It cannot: `quote(now, rp, inventory)` takes the reference price and
the share count as **arguments**. Our build is what computes both. His RPV-2 is
the same — `step(now, fair_value)` is handed the fair value too.

So nothing in his package replaces our build. But **one idea in it is better
than ours and we should take it**: the spread follows measured price movement
instead of a lookup table keyed on a state classifier we have not built.

This document says exactly what changes, in the order a builder needs it.

## The one rule that governs every choice below

Per [[market-maker/decisions]] (30-07): **the spec's finance is authoritative;
its engineering is AI scaffolding.** The same filter applies to this package,
harder. It is code generated by a domain expert who does not code. Judge the
*ideas* on merit; do not inherit the *implementation*.

Concretely, three things in his package must never be ported:

- **`float` everywhere.** §1.6-3 makes Decimal authoritative. Port, never lift.
- **`time.time()` inside the model.** §10.3 requires bit-identical replay. Every
  `Δt` comes from event timestamps.
- **`random.Random`.** §5.7.3's seeded SHA-256 scheme is the one we reproduced
  byte-exact. Randomness stays on that path.

## 0 · The assembly — how the pieces chain (added 30-07 evening)

The full pipeline for one quote, agreed with George:

```
RP      the model price (Chapter 3, built — unchanged)
  ↓
+ lean  slide down when long, up when short (§4.5/§4.6, built)
  ↓
centre  the Reservation Midpoint (RM)
  ↓
± width/2   the spread, from the volatility equation (§3 below)
  ↓
L1 bid / L1 ask
  ↓
± step      ladder levels outward (§5 below)
```

The lean moves the pair; the width sets the gap. When the width is an odd
number of ticks, one side carries the extra tick — alternate the side per
shape draw.

**Two proposals attached to this assembly, both pending Edwin — build without
them, structure the code so they slot in:**

1. **Split-position lean.** `traded = NP − OpeningPosition`; lean on `traded`
   with a small cap (sentiment) and on the mandated part with its own cap
   (distribution, Edwin sets it). **Cannot be built until E27 answers what
   OpeningPosition is.** Until then the §4.5 single-position lean stands.
2. **Size/depth asymmetry as the distribution tool.** While the mandated
   position is large: more levels and much larger sizes on the offer side,
   fewer and smaller on the bid. Prices stay fair; no invariant is touched.
   Needs: the §5.7.3 quantity ceiling raised (15,000 binds first), the §5.7.2
   modifier range widened past 1.5×, and §5.2's symmetric level counts
   relaxed. All three are InPlay numbers → E31.

⚠ **Rejected after review (30-07 evening): blending market sentiment into RP
itself.** The idea was RP = w × model + (1−w) × participant-only price (§5.5),
w falling as volume grows. Killed for three reasons: the model's forward leg is
already anchored to de-vigged **sportsbook** lines — a far deeper crowd than
our book; resting orders are free, so a §5.5-driven mark is **spoofable**, and
§4.2 marks every portfolio (and the leaderboard) at RP; and §2.3 exists
precisely to keep the mark clean — Edwin's rule is deliberate, not an
oversight. Season 1 collects the tape; revisit with evidence. E34 is asked as
a product-intent question only.

## 1 · Reference price — NO CHANGE, and do not build his

**What we have.** `mm/valuation/reference_price.py` — Edwin's own on-field leg:

```
RP = $5 × ( T − Σ p_ref(g) + Σ x_g )  +  off-field terms
```

over G, the games kicked off since T's `effective_time`. Built, 70 tests.

**What he proposes.** RPV-2 publishes:

```
published RP = fair value
             + trend        slow random OU drift, capped ±80 ticks (±$0.80)
             + random walk   continuous Gaussian shocks, state-scaled
             + event jumps   a decaying step on a game event
             + flow impact   6 ticks per 1,000 net shares bought
             then clamped to a band around fair value
```

**Ruling: build none of it.** Reasons, in order of strength:

1. **Three of the four additions are invented movement.** The trend and the
   random walk are not information. His own header states the purpose: *"the
   reference price sits still and the noise taker just chops around a fixed
   anchor… RPV-1 makes RP MOVE… so the whole market breathes."* That is a
   simulator making a test market look alive.
2. **The event jumps are a substitute for something we already have.** A
   simulator has no probability feed, so it fakes the effect of a touchdown
   with a jump. We take Sportradar's live win probability directly — `x_g`
   already moves the price on every play. Adding a jump would double-count it.
3. **The flow impact is the compliance problem.** RP would rise because someone
   bought, and on day one the house taker (SNT-1) is doing most of the buying.
   One house account moves the published price; the other quotes against it.
   That needs Troy and a compliance view, not an engineering decision.
4. **His own document disagrees with his own code** on the coefficient — 10
   ticks per 1,000 shares in `HANDOFF.md` §3, `6.0` in `RPV2Config`.

**Blocked on:** **E30** — is RPV-1/RPV-2 the price we publish to participants,
or the price generator inside his simulation? Until he answers, Chapter 3
stands unchanged.

## 2 · The volatility number — NEW, and it is the core of the change

Everything below depends on one figure, `σ²`. It is the only state the model
carries per security, and it is what replaces the state classifier.

### State

One value per security: **`V`**, the variance rate, in **ticks² per second**.
Plus the previous RP and its timestamp, both of which we already hold.

### The update, on every Reference Price publication

```
1 · the move, in ticks       r  = |RP − RP_prev| ÷ tick

2 · decay what you had       V  ← V · exp( −ln2 · Δt ÷ h )

3 · what this move says      v  = r² ÷ Δt

4 · blend, asymmetrically    V  ← v                 if v > V
                             V  ← 0.5·V + 0.5·v     otherwise

5 · scale and clamp          σ² = clamp( V · H, floor, ceil )
```

| Symbol | Meaning | Value | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| `RP`, `RP_prev` | this and the previous reference price | — | — |
| `Δt` | seconds between them, **from event timestamps** | — | — |
| `tick` | price increment | $0.01 | ✅ §5.3 |
| `h` | decay half-life | 20 s | 🟡 his |
| `H` | horizon | 30 s | 🟡 his |
| `floor` | minimum σ², so the spread cannot collapse | 0.05 ticks² | 🟡 his |
| `ceil` | maximum σ², so a jump cannot blow the spread out | 400 ticks² | 🟡 his |
| `σ²` | variance over the horizon | derived | — |

### Four implementation notes the code will get wrong otherwise

- **`H` is applied once, in step 5.** The width formula in §3 below uses `σ²`
  already scaled by `H`. Do not multiply by `H` again — the canonical
  Avellaneda-Stoikov risk term is `γσ²(T−t)`, and the `(T−t)` is already inside.
- **Step 4 is deliberately asymmetric.** A bigger move than the running average
  replaces it outright; a smaller one pulls it only half way down. So the book
  widens instantly on news and comes back over roughly 20 seconds. Keep it.
- **`Δt` never comes from a clock.** Both timestamps are already in the event
  envelope (§7.1). Replay depends on this.
- **Decimal has `.exp()` and `.ln()`.** No float is needed for step 2. If the
  per-cycle cost matters across 170 securities, cache the decay factor per
  distinct `Δt` bucket — do not switch to float to save time.

### Replay

`σ²` is derived entirely from the journalled RP sequence and its timestamps, so
it needs no event type of its own and replay reproduces it exactly. Nothing to
add to §7.3. *(Contrast with **N23**, where `T` genuinely does need one.)*

### What it produces, on our own measured data

The 4-second gap is the median Sportradar probability update interval measured
on the Chiefs–Ravens capture (24-07), not an assumption.

| Situation | Move | Gap | σ² | Width |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quiet book, no game | 1 tick | 30 s | 1.0 | 2 ticks |
| Live game, ordinary play | 5 ticks | 4 s | 188 | 6 ticks |
| Touchdown | 20 ticks | 4 s | **400 (capped)** | 10 ticks |

⚠ **The ceiling binds on any real scoring play.** Past a touchdown-sized move
the width stops responding. See the open item in §3.

## 3 · The width — REPLACES the §5.2 lookup table

**What the spec says.** Classify the book Stable / Active / Defensive, then read
the spread off a table: $0.10 / $0.20 / $0.40, with 3 / 2 / 1 levels. Something
upstream must decide the state. **That classifier was never built** (N3's
thresholds remain 🔴).

**What replaces it.**

```
risk_width  = γ · σ²  +  C

              where C = (2/γ) · ln(1 + γ/k)

full_width  = ceil( clamp(risk_width, min_width, max_width)
                    + extra × price_scale )

price_scale = clamp( RP ÷ ref_price, 0.6, 1.6 )
extra       ∈ [0, 3] ticks, seeded, held for the dwell
```

| Parameter | Meaning | His value | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| `γ` | risk aversion | 0.02 | 🔴 **E31** — his or ours |
| `k` | order-flow intensity; larger → tighter | 1.2 | 🔴 **E31** |
| `C` | the constant floor term | **1.653 ticks** | derived |
| `extra` | random widening above the risk floor | 0–3 ticks | 🟡 |
| `ref_price` | price at which `extra` is unscaled | $65.00 | 🟡 |
| `min_width` / `max_width` | bounds | 1 / 60 ticks | 🔴 **E31** |

**Compute `C` once at construction.** `γ` and `k` are constants, so the
logarithm is a constant — 1.653 ticks. Evaluating `ln` 170 times a cycle for a
fixed number is the kind of thing his code does and we should not.

**Additive, not maximum.** He changed this deliberately and says why: taking
`max(random, risk)` let the random draw hide the risk widening, so a volatile
book looked identical to a calm one. Adding them means the risk term always
shows. Keep additive.

**Splitting across the mid.** `bid_off = full_width // 2`,
`ask_off = full_width − bid_off`, alternating which side carries the odd tick
on each redraw.

### ⚠ Open — the width has no wide end

His σ² ceiling of 400 caps the width at about **10 ticks plus the random 3**.
On a $65 team that is $0.13, ever. Our own numbers go far wider:

- §5.2 Defensive is **$0.40**
- the indicated overnight spread (20-07, Troy) is **$2.50 – $5.00**

So the equation cannot express the two states where we most want to be wide: a
degraded feed, and an overnight book with no game and no information. Two ways
out, and Edwin picks:

1. Raise the σ² ceiling and let the equation reach those widths, or
2. Keep a per-state **floor** on the width — Defensive and Overnight set a
   minimum the equation may exceed but never undercut.

**Recommendation: option 2.** A stale feed is not a volatility event. σ² would
be *low* precisely when the input has died, so the equation would quote tight
into exactly the situation §2.3 says is dangerous. The freshness ladder (§3.3,
§3.4) already detects it. Wire that to a width floor. → **E31**.

## 4 · The lean — KEEP OURS, take one idea, and note it makes N20 worse

**What we have.** `inventory.py`, §4.3–§4.6, built and tested:

```
PR = NetPosition ÷ ReferenceFloat            ReferenceFloat = issued − treasury
IA = −clamp( PR × S, −M, +M )                S = $1.00, M = $0.25
RM = clamp( RP + IA, $0.01, MEV )
```

**What he proposes.**

```
q    = NetPosition ÷ 4,000
lean = −clamp( q × 10 ticks × (σ² ÷ 25), ±30 ticks )
```

**Ruling: keep our denominator. His is wrong for us.**

Dividing by the float answers the question we actually care about — *what share
of this team do we own?* Dividing by 4,000 answers *how many times a normal
market-maker position are we holding?*, and 4,000 has no relationship to the
security. The two saturate at wildly different points:

| | Denominator | Cap | Cap reached at |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ours | 900,000 (NFL float) | $0.25 | 225,000 sh |
| His | 4,000 | $0.30 | 12,000 sh |

Every extra share moves his lean about **22×** further than ours, so his ceiling
arrives about **19×** sooner. ⚠ Note the correction of 30-07: our day-one
holding is **not** a fixed 500,000 — the Mandate is "buy whatever participants
do not", so it depends on demand. But the argument does not rest on the number.
Even a **90 % subscribed** offering leaves us ~50,000 shares a team, still **8×**
his one-sided threshold and **4×** his cap.

**Ruling: do NOT take his volatility scaling on the lean.** Take it on the width
only. Reasoning, and this matters:

- The width is a **risk** control. Scaling it with volatility is correct — a
  fast-moving price is genuinely more dangerous to quote against.
- The lean is a **distribution** tool, not a risk tool (decisions, 29-07: §1.5
  excludes profit and we have unlimited capital, so the reason to shed inventory
  is that a market with no shares in circulation is not a market).
- Scaling a distribution tool by volatility means we push hardest to distribute
  during a live game and least overnight, which is backwards — and it drives the
  lean into its cap *sooner*, so **it makes N20 worse, not better**.

**Still unresolved: N20.** Neither design can distribute. `M` must rise, or
distribution is not our job. Edwin has not answered. `M` stays 🔴.

**Net change to `inventory.py`: none required today.** §4.3–§4.6 stand as built.

## 5 · The ladder — take the shape, keep our scale and our seeding

**What the spec says.** Levels 3 / 2 / 1 by state; offsets ±$0.05 then ±$0.10;
base quantities 10,000 / 7,500 / 5,000; ±25 % seeded variation (§5.7.3),
increment 500, bounds 1,000–15,000.

**What he proposes.** Levels 3–6 drawn at random; step 1–4 ticks drawn at
random; sizes 250 × 0.72ⁱ with ±35 % jitter; the whole shape redrawn on a dwell
timer — 3–12 s live, 8–30 s pre-kickoff, 10–40 s post, 20–90 s overnight.

| | Adopt? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Random level count 3–6 | ✅ yes | Removes another dependency on the unbuilt classifier; a varying level count reads less programmatic |
| Random step 1–4 ticks | ✅ yes | Same reason. Bounded, harmless |
| Geometric size decay ×0.72 | ✅ yes | Cleaner than three hard-coded numbers, and extends to any level count |
| Base size 250 | ✂ **no** | 40× too small. Keep the spec's 10,000 scale |
| ±35 % jitter via `random.Random` | ✂ **no** | Keep §5.7.3's seeded SHA-256 scheme — it is the fixture we reproduced byte-exact and the reason replay works |
| Dwell redraw on a timer | ⚠ **gate it** | See below |

### ⚠ The dwell timer must not drive a requote on its own

A redraw every 3–12 seconds in a live game means cancelling and reposting the
whole ladder **with no new information**, on 170 securities. Two problems:

1. **Message budget.** T2 (`MaxOrdRate`) is still unanswered.
2. **It contradicts the lifecycle.** Under rest-until-gone (Edwin, 23-07 — still
   in conflict with §5.9 as **E17**), a redraw wipes a partially-filled level
   and reposts the remainder at a fresh queue position, for cosmetic reasons.

**Rule: the dwell defines when the shape is allowed to change, not when we
requote.** §5.8's material-change thresholds ($0.005 on IA, 500 shares on
quantity) remain the gate. If nothing material moved, hold the book. When
something does move, and the dwell has expired, draw a new shape at the same
time. → filed as **N26** (ours, engineering).

## 6 · The guards — reject two, keep ours

| His guard | Value | Ruling |
|---|---|---|
| Shrink the risk side | past 8,000 sh | ✅ **already ours** — §5.7.2's position-side modifier (0.50–1.50×) does the same job, off the Effective Position Ratio, and is properly specified |
| **Quote one-sided** | past 6,000 sh in LIVE | ✂ **REJECT** |
| **Halt the book** | $25,000 MTM drawdown | ✂ **REJECT** |
| Requote throttle | 2 s / 2 ticks / 300 sh | ✅ **already ours** — §5.8 material-change thresholds, better specified |
| Price bounds | — | ✅ **ours only** — §5.4, $0.01 floor to MEV ceiling. He has no equivalent; keep the §4.6 clamp |

**Why the one-sided guard is rejected.** §4.1 is explicit: *inventory never
prevents quoting.* That rule is correct for us specifically, because we are the
mandated buyer of the residual float. His threshold is 6,000 shares. Even a
lightly-unsold offering puts us many times past it, and the consequence is that
**every book quotes sell-only with no bid anywhere in the market**. His largest
tested position across 400 simulated seasons was 5,900 shares.

**Why the drawdown kill is rejected.** Halting a book we are mandated to make is
not a risk control; it is a market outage. §1.5 excludes profit as a motive, so
mark-to-market drawdown is not a signal we should act on at all. The kill switch
we do need already exists — §6.3, global, operator-triggered, executing through
the gateway's `cancel_all`.
