How Markets Create Price

The price at which that transaction happens becomes the new reference point for the entire market\.

This is why InPlay's single liquidity pool is a critical structural advantage\. In fragmented markets like sports betting, every bookmaker sets their own lines\. There is no unified price, no real price discovery, and no way to see what the market truly believes\. Outcome Dependent vs\. Path Dependent: The Core Distinction

This is one of the most important concepts in this entire guide\. Understanding it will change how you think about every trade you place\.

Gambling is outcome dependent\. You place a bet on a result\. Either the result happens or it does not\. Nothing you do between placing the bet and the outcome being decided changes what happens\. There is no way to adapt, no decision to make mid\-event, and no skill that improves your result once the wager is placed\. Your fate is entirely tied to a single binary outcome\.

Trading is path dependent\. Your profit or loss is not determined by whether a team wins or loses\. It is determined by where the price was when you entered, where it moved while you held the position, and where it was when you exited\. Two traders can both hold a long position on the same team during the same game and have completely different outcomes depending on when they entered, how they sized the position, whether they used a stop\-loss, and when they chose to exit\.

How a Trade Works: The Full Cycle

Every trade goes through the same fundamental cycle\. Understanding each stage makes you a more deliberate, disciplined trader\.

__1__

__Thesis Formation__

Before anything else, you develop a reason to trade\. This is your thesis: a specific, evidence\-based belief about where the price is going and why\. 'I think the Chiefs offense will outperform this week because their matchup favors their running game and the opposing defense is missing two starters\.'

__2__

__Entry__

You open a position by executing a buy \(long\) or sell \(short\) order\. You choose your order type based on whether speed or price precision matters more in this moment\.

__3__

__Position Management__

You monitor the trade as conditions evolve\. New information comes in during the game\. You decide whether it reinforces your thesis, weakens it, or invalidates it entirely\. Adjust accordingly\.

__4__

__Exit__

You close the position by executing the opposing order\. If you went long, you sell\. If you went short, you buy back\. You exit at your profit target, your stop\-loss level, or when your thesis no longer holds\.

__5__

__Review__

After the trade, you record what happened and why\. Was your thesis correct? Did you execute your plan? What would you do differently? This is where improvement actually happens\.

Understanding P&L: Profit and Loss

Your P&L \(Profit and Loss\) is the running total of all realized and unrealized gains and losses across your positions\. It is the most direct measure of how you are performing as a trader\.

__Unrealized P&L__

The profit or loss on positions you currently hold but have not yet closed\. If you bought at 45\.00 and the price is now 50\.00, your unrealized P&L is \+5\.00 per unit\. It is not real money until you close the position\.

__Realized P&L__

The profit or loss from positions you have already closed\. Once you sell, the gain or loss is locked in\. This is actual money added to or subtracted from your account\.

__Mark\-to\-Market__

Valuing your open positions at the current market price rather than the price you paid\. InPlay's live leaderboard uses mark\-to\-market to show real\-time P&L rankings\.

In the 2026 InPlay Trading Challenge, the live leaderboard ranks competitors by P&L in real time\. Top performers are visible to all participants\. The leaderboard itself becomes a market signal, showing which traders are reading the game correctly and creating competitive pressure that drives engagement throughout the season\.

The Mechanics of Market Participation

When you place a trade on InPlay, you are interacting with the market in one of three ways:

__Taking Liquidity__

You place a market order and execute immediately at the current bid or ask\. You are taking liquidity from the market by transacting against an existing order\. You pay the spread for this immediacy\.

__Adding Liquidity__

You place a limit order that sits in the order book waiting to be filled\. You are adding liquidity by making your willingness to trade at a specific price visible to the market\. You avoid paying the spread by waiting for the market to come to you\.

__Order Book__

The live list of all pending buy \(bid\) and sell \(ask\) orders in the market at different price levels\. It shows you where other traders are positioned and how deep the market is at each price\.

__ORDER TYPES__

__VIDEO__

__Watch the Module 7 video before reading this section\. __Video lessons are available in the InPlay Learning Center inside the app\.

An order is just an instruction to the market\. You are telling the platform: buy this, sell that, or do it only if the price hits a specific level\. Understanding order types gives you precise control over how and when your trades execute\. InPlay's single liquidity pool means all traders are trading in the same market, so order execution is efficient and prices are competitive\.

Market Order

A market order executes immediately at whatever the current price is\. You are saying: buy this right now, whatever it costs\. Market orders are fast and guarantee execution, but in fast\-moving markets you might pay a slightly different price than expected because prices can shift in the seconds it takes to fill\. This is called slippage\.

__When to Use It__

Use a market order when speed matters more than precision\. If a volatility moment is unfolding on the field and you need to get into a position immediately, a market order is the right call\.

Limit Order

A limit order lets you set the exact price you are willing to buy or sell at\. The trade only executes if the market reaches that price\. If you want to buy at 46\.00 on a position currently at 50\.00, your limit order sits waiting\. If the price hits 46\.00, your order fills\. If it never gets there, the order does not execute and you owe nothing\.

__When to Use It__

Use limit orders when you have a specific price in mind and you are not in a rush\. Pre\-season and pre\-game positioning is ideal for limit orders\. Set your entry level and let the market come to you\.

Stop Order \(Stop\-Loss\)

A stop order is your safety mechanism\. You set a trigger price, and if the market hits it, the order automatically executes\. The most common use is a stop\-loss on an open position\. If you buy at 50\.00 and want to limit your loss to 10%, you set a stop\-loss at 45\.00\. If the price drops to 45\.00, your position is automatically sold with no manual action required\.

Stop orders can protect both long and short positions\. On a short position, a stop order triggers if the price rises to a specified level, automatically closing the position before your losses compound during a momentum reversal\.

__When to Use It__

Always\. Especially when you are new\. Live sports markets can move fast when momentum shifts\. A stop\-loss is what keeps a bad quarter from becoming a catastrophic session\.

Technical analysis is the practice of studying historical price movements to forecast where prices might go next\. Price movement is not random\. Patterns emerge\. Trends establish themselves\. Momentum builds and fades\. Reading a Price Chart

A price chart is a visual record of where a security has traded over time\. The horizontal axis is time\. The vertical axis is price\. Every point on the chart tells you what the market agreed a position was worth at that moment\.

__Trend__

The general direction prices are moving\. Over a series of moves, prices tend to be heading up, down, or sideways\.

__Uptrend__

A pattern of higher highs and higher lows\. Each peak is higher than the last\. Each dip is higher than the previous\. Buyers are in control\.

__Downtrend__

A pattern of lower highs and lower lows\. Each bounce fails at a lower level\. Sellers are in control\.

__Consolidation__

Price is moving in a range without clear direction\. Often precedes a significant move when momentum returns to the game\.

Support and Resistance

Support is a price level where buying interest has historically been strong enough to stop a price decline and push it back up\. Think of it as a floor\. Resistance is the opposite, a ceiling where selling pressure has consistently stopped price rallies\.

When a price breaks through support decisively, that former support often becomes the new resistance\. This flip is called a role reversal\. Recognizing it early, especially around major game events, is a genuine trading edge\.

__InPlay Application__

The performance index will often oscillate around certain price levels before a bigger move\. A team holding at 45\.00 after multiple dips is showing support there\. If it breaks below convincingly after a key turnover or injury, the long trade thesis needs to be reassessed immediately\.

Volatility Moments and Market Surges

On InPlay, the most valuable moments in sports are also the most valuable moments for traders\. Volatility moments, sudden shifts with high emotion and maximum attention, create the clearest technical signals\. These include game\-winning scores, quarterback injuries, defensive turnovers, and momentum\-shifting plays\.

Market surges happen when momentum builds and more traders pile into a position\. Recognizing the early stages of a surge, when momentum is creating movement and volume is confirming it, is where technical analysis pays off most directly in a live sports market\.

Momentum and Volume

Momentum measures the speed of price movement, not just the direction\. Volume is the number of units being traded\. High volume on a move confirms it\. Low volume on a move raises a flag that it may not hold\.

__The Key Principle__

Technical analysis does not predict the future with certainty\. It is a tool for assessing probabilities\. The more signals that align, trend, support, momentum, volume, the higher your confidence should be\. No single indicator is enough on its own\.

Every major trading institution treats psychology as one of the most critical topics in trader education\. There is a reason for that\. Most traders who lose money do not lose it because they lack knowledge\. They lose it because of how they behave under pressure\.

In a live sports market, this problem is amplified\. You are watching a game you care about\. The crowd is loud\. Momentum shifts are sudden\. A fumble recovery or a big completion changes the emotional temperature of millions of viewers simultaneously, and traders are not immune to that\. Understanding your psychological responses to these moments is as important as any technical or fundamental analysis you do\.

The Six Psychological Traps

__01  FOMO: Fear of Missing Out__

A position starts moving and you were not in it\. The urge to chase it, to buy in late at a higher price just to participate, is FOMO\. In a live sports market, this often happens right after a momentum shift when prices have already moved significantly\.

__How to counter it__

If you missed the entry, you missed it\. Look for the next setup\. Chasing a move that has already happened dramatically shifts the risk/reward ratio against you\.

__02  Loss Aversion__

Humans feel the pain of a loss roughly twice as intensely as the pleasure of an equivalent gain\. In sports trading, this can mean holding a losing position through an entire quarter hoping the team turns it around, when the data clearly says otherwise\.

__How to counter it__

Predetermine your exit points before you enter\. Stop\-losses handle loss aversion mechanically\. Let the rules make the decision, not the emotion\.

__03  Overconfidence__

A strong run of winning trades can create the feeling that you have figured the market out\. This is when traders increase position sizes, skip research, and drop stop\-losses\. That is when the market corrects the assumption\.

__How to counter it__

Keep records\. Review wins and losses systematically\. The live sports market is dynamic\. What worked in Week 4 may not work in Week 12\.

__04  Paralysis by Analysis__

The opposite of overconfidence\. Overthinking a setup until the opportunity passes\. In a fast\-moving live market, hesitation is costly\. A great trade at a good price beats a perfect trade never taken\.

__How to counter it__

Have pre\-defined entry criteria and act when they are met\. Preparation before the game is what allows confident execution during it\.

__05  Anchoring__

Fixating on a specific price point, usually what you paid, and making decisions relative to it rather than current conditions\. The market does not care what you paid\. It only cares about what is happening right now\.

__How to counter it__

Judge every position based on current conditions\. Ask: if I had no position right now, would I enter one at this price? If no, consider exiting\.

__06  Confirmation Bias__

Seeking out information that supports what you already want to do and ignoring information that contradicts it\. If you are bullish on a team, you will naturally discount bad injury news or poor matchup data\.

__How to counter it__

Actively look for the argument against your trade before entering it\. If the counterargument is weak, proceed\. If it is strong, size down\.

__Bottom Line__

You cannot eliminate emotion from trading\. What you can do is build systems, checklists, rules, predefined entry and exit levels, that reduce how much emotional noise affects your decisions\. Valuation Anchors: What Is Fair Value?

One of the questions every trader asks is whether the current price is fair, too high, or too low\. In traditional markets, analysts build complex discounted cash flow models to answer this\. On InPlay, fair value is determined by a combination of the factors below\.

__Historical Range__

__Current Game Context__

__Relative Performance__

How is the team performing relative to their pre\-game expectations? A team outperforming expectations should trade at a premium\. A team underperforming should trade at a discount, regardless of their historical level\.

__Momentum Trajectory__

Is performance improving or deteriorating? A team that has converted four consecutive third downs and is driving toward the red zone has a different fair value than a team that just punted three times in a row, even if the current index value is similar\.

Blockchain sounds technical\. It is not\. Once you understand what problem it solves, the concept clicks quickly\.

Imagine a spreadsheet that records every financial transaction, who bought what, who sold it, at what price, and when\. Now imagine that spreadsheet is not stored on one computer\. Identical copies are stored on thousands of computers around the world simultaneously\.

Every time a new transaction happens, it gets added to every copy at the same time\. No single person or company controls the ledger\. And once a transaction is recorded, it cannot be changed without rewriting it on every computer in the network simultaneously, which is effectively impossible\. That is a blockchain\. A shared, permanent, tamper\-resistant record of transactions\.

How the Chain Actually Works

__1__

__A transaction occurs__

Someone buys or sells a Performance Security\. That transaction is broadcast to the network\.

__2__

__The transaction is verified__

Computers on the network \(called nodes\) confirm the transaction is legitimate\.

__3__

__It gets grouped into a block__

Multiple verified transactions are packaged together into a single block of data\.

__4__

__The block is added to the chain__

That block is permanently linked to the one before it, forming a chronological chain\.

__5__

__The record is final__

Once confirmed, no one can alter or delete that block without breaking the entire chain\.
