## The Roof — Master Idea

**OWNERSHIP, NOT OUTCOMES.**

The category we are creating: **sports investing**. Not a better bet. Not a legal alternative to anything. A new asset class — defined on its own terms, in its own vocabulary.

### Positioning statement

For people who analyze the game, InPlay is the sports investing market where you own shares in operating companies that earn real revenue — because a share is a claim on what a business earns, not a position on a result.

### Boilerplate — use verbatim

> InPlay Global operates a sports investing market: exchange-style trading in shares of operating companies built around professional and college football — real businesses with income statements, priced by buyers and sellers. Ownership, not outcomes.

## The Foundation — Core Narrative

A bettor's return has one source: the outcome. A shareholder's return comes from what an enterprise earns — revenue built across an entire schedule of play and the commercial business around it, aggregated in an income statement, priced continuously by buyers and sellers. That is why a team can lose on Sunday and its stock can trade up on Monday: the price reflects the earning power of the business, not the last final score. InPlay is built on the earnings side of that line.

**Usage rule:** this passage never appears in consumer copy — the category is defined on its own terms there. Deploy in founder commentary, analyst briefings, and press conversations only, and always as product structure, never as a claim about anyone's legality.

## Three Pillars — Audience, Message, Proof

### Pillar 1 — The Analytical Fan (beachhead)

- **Promise:** Your football knowledge finally has an asset class.
- **Message:** You have been doing equity research your whole life — film, stats, roster construction — with no equity at the end of it. Now the analysis ends in ownership.
- **Proof:** team-company income statements; public listings you can buy and sell; research-grade transparency — the revenue drivers play on national television.
- **Channels:** football-analytics X and Reddit, podcasts, founder threads, the InPlay Trading Challenge as the on-ramp.
- **Voice applied (sample post):** "You've been charting efficiency stats for a decade for free. Now the same film study is research on a company whose revenue drivers play on Sunday. Ownership, not outcomes."
- **Never write:** "Get in before kickoff and cash out big when your team covers."

### Pillar 2 — The Modern Investor

- **Promise:** The most transparent companies you will ever analyze.
- **Message:** Real operating companies, market-priced shares, and a business you can watch operate every single week.
- **Proof:** exchange-style pricing — no house, no odds, no one on the other side of your money but another investor; fractional access; a brokerage-shaped experience end to end.
- **Channels:** fintech media, investing creators, app-store presence, referral-share mechanics (brokerage-style, never bonus-shaped).
- **Voice applied (sample line):** "Real operating companies, priced by buyers and sellers. No house, no odds — no one on the other side of your money but another investor."
- **Never write:** "A risk-free way to win money on football every Sunday."

### Pillar 3 — Media & Sophisticated Observers

- **Promise:** A new category with a century of precedent.
- **Message:** Securities markets have always housed instruments whose economics ride uncertain events — catastrophe bonds, biotech equity, film financing, even DraftKings stock. The category test has never been certainty of revenue; it is what the holder owns.
- **Proof:** founder commentary (ex-CBOT market structure), the comparator ladder, the structure described factually and without legal conclusions.
- **Channels:** earned media, founder LinkedIn and X, conference panels, analyst briefings. **This pillar is founder-voice only.**
- **Voice applied (sample founder line):** "Catastrophe bonds ride hurricanes and they're securities. The category test was never certainty of revenue — it's what the holder owns."
- **Never write:** "Unlike the gambling platforms in the news, InPlay is fully approved and legal."

## Proof-Point Bank (in order of use)

1. **Sunday/Monday — the hero.** A team can lose the game and its stock can trade up, because the price reflects the earning power of the enterprise, not the last score. At launch, show it live: clip + thread + explainer the first time it happens.
2. **A claim on a business.** Shareholders hold transferable equity in operating companies with income statements — many games, many contracts, many revenue lines. No game result triggers a payment to any shareholder.
3. **No house.** Buyers and sellers set every price. No book, no odds, no vig — the market matches investors with each other.
4. **Built to earn on the field and off.** Performance revenue across an entire schedule plus commercial revenue by design. Use design language until commercial results exist; every signed sponsorship graduates this from design to fact — announce each one loudly.
5. **The comparator ladder (sophisticated audiences only).** Catastrophe bonds, biotech equity, film-slate financing, DraftKings stock: securities whose economics hinge on uncertain events. What the holder owns — not certainty of revenue — has always defined the category.

## Red Lines — Non-Negotiable

- Never state or imply regulator approval or endorsement. Qualification is not approval.
- No claims about current revenue mix ahead of the income statement. Design language ("built to earn on the field and off") until commercial results exist.
- Shareholders own equity in team companies — never the franchise, never league IP, no implied league affiliation.
- No betting-shaped promotions: no deposit-match bonuses, no "risk-free" mechanics, no free-bet analogs. Brokerage-pattern mechanics — referral shares, fractional-share promotions — are the approved shape.
- No named competitors and no legal conclusions in public copy. Reference the moment obliquely: "as regulators draw lines around prediction markets…"
- No performance promises, return expectations, or outcome guarantees. Ever.
- Skill-versus-chance is not our argument. Never lead with "sports is skill." Our line: it is not about how skilled the participant is — it is about what the participant owns.
- All public materials use official IPG logos and brand colors, and receive legal review before publication.

## Appendix — Objection Handling & Product Contrast

**Audience gate:** internal, founder commentary, analyst briefings, and press conversations only. This material never appears in consumer copy — the category is defined on its own terms there — and is always deployed as product structure, never as a claim about anyone's legality.

A fair word on prediction markets, because the contrast defines the category: a sharp trader can make money in them. The product simply caps what the work is worth. Every contract is binary — right or wrong, a dollar or zero — so the maximum payout is fixed before the trade is ever placed. Fees and spreads are charged against edges that thin. Every event must be underwritten again from scratch — the analysis never compounds. And the market is only alive while the question is open: the moment an outcome becomes obvious, the price pins near certainty and the other side of the trade disappears — you can be exactly right and unable to exit at fair value. Equity inverts all of it. A share's value has no predetermined ceiling, because it tracks what the enterprise earns. One piece of research covers an entire operating business, not a single question. And certainty doesn't kill the market — a decided game simply posts to the income statement as earned revenue, and the market moves on to pricing the rest of the business. Skilled analysis deserves a product without a lid on it.

**Mirror question (counsel sign-off required before any external use):** "Doesn't your market thin out when a team's season is decided?" Directional answer only: a decided game becomes booked revenue rather than a dead contract, and shares keep their claim on everything the company has already earned. Do not improvise mechanics — no statements about price convergence, distributions, or market-maker obligations.
