## About This Document

Canonical source of truth for the audiences InPlay is being built for. Component documents reference this doc rather than restating audience descriptions — each component contains a **per-component usage table** (how each audience uses *that* component and what they need from it), not full audience bios.

Edwin's overall target: _"an 18 to 55 year old who loves sports and wants to try to make some money engaging with it."_ He explicitly resisted narrow segmentation. The four audiences below are not hard segments — they are useful archetypes that surface different motivations, channels, and product behaviours.

## Audience 1: The Crypto-Savvy Sports Trader (The Early Adopter)

> **One-liner:** Crypto-fluent, prediction-market-frustrated, hunting for true price discovery in sports.

**Their World:** They live on X (Twitter) and Discord. They understand prediction markets (Polymarket, Kalshi) and crypto charts just as well as they understand NFL depth charts. They think in terms of "EV" (Expected Value) and momentum.

**Their Pains:** While prediction markets are an evolution from betting, they are still frustratingly binary (Yes/No). They don't offer the continuous price discovery, fluid trading mechanics, or momentum-riding that true crypto or equity markets do.

**Their Fears:** Being trapped in binary outcomes where they can't dynamically trade in and out of a position as a team's performance evolves over a game or season.

**Their Secret Hope:** To find a platform with _true price discovery_ for sports — where they can trade performance fluidly, just like they trade altcoins.

**Demographic notes:**
- Age skews 22–35
- US-based, mobile-first, comfortable with Coinbase / Robinhood-style KYC
- May want crypto payouts (Coinbase wallet linking flagged in [[components/customer-onboarding/customer-onboarding]])

**Acquisition channels:** X / Discord communities, prediction-market subreddits, crypto-trading newsletters, podcasts.

**Why they switch to InPlay:** Path-dependent pricing replaces the binary trap. Single liquidity pool. Real equity mechanics they can trust. Continuous trading throughout the game and season.

## Audience 2: The Analytical Fan / "Armchair GM" (The Broad Target)

> **One-liner:** Deep sports knowledge with no way to monetise it beyond fantasy or basic parlays.

**Their World:** Deep into fantasy football, Reddit debates, and sports podcasts. They are the friend in the group chat who calls a team a "fraud" three weeks before the team actually collapses.

**Their Pains:** They have a massive wealth of sports knowledge, but no way to truly capitalise on it outside of a season-long fantasy league or a basic parlay.

**Their Fears:** Being proven exactly right about a team's trajectory, but having nothing to show for it except an "I told you so."

**Their Secret Hope:** To have a definitive, public arena that proves they are smarter than the "casuals" and the consensus market.

**Demographic notes:**
- Age 25–45, broad income range (truck drivers through office workers)
- Edwin: _"I want to reach truck drivers who really know football and don't have a way to express that other than gambling"_
- Not finance-educated; familiar with sports betting apps

**Acquisition channels:** Friend referrals, social shares, gorilla marketing (t-shirt QR, viewing parties), fantasy and sports subreddits, sports podcasts.

**Why they switch to InPlay:** Sports knowledge translates directly into a tradable edge. Cash prizes for being right. No structural house edge. A nuanced way to express a multi-day view rather than a binary bet.

## Audience 3: The Finance-Curious Student (The Campus Buzz)

> **One-liner:** Ambitious, social, treats trading like a skill to build — not a gamble.

**Their World:** College campuses, Robinhood, Barstool Sports. They are ambitious, social, and looking for the next big cultural wave. Sports are central to their weekend rituals.

**Their Pains:** The market is drowning in "me-too" prediction platforms. Furthermore, traditional financial markets feel opaque and intimidating, leaving their deep sports knowledge as an untapped asset. They want to trade, but they want to trade what they actually _know_.

**Their Fears:** Settling for the status quo and missing out on the chance to be part of the _actual_ next big wave in culture and finance.

**Their Secret Hope:** To discover a genuinely new asset class where their native language (sports) translates directly into market power, allowing them to be the early-adopter tastemaker in their friend group.

**Demographic notes:**
- Age 18–25
- Includes international students at US universities (Taiwan, Vietnam, China, India)
- Generation comfortable with mobile-first financial products and meme-stock-style risk-taking
- Doesn't have the capital to trade real markets (Edwin's own youth: _"one guy told me if I had 50 grand, he'd let me trade with him. I didn't have 50 grand"_)

**Acquisition channels:** Campus ambassadors, dot cards, alumni networks, university curriculum integration (5 universities confirmed, target 10–15), TikTok / Instagram.

**Why they switch to InPlay:** Zero cost to enter. Real cash prizes. Builds real financial literacy that transfers to traditional equity markets. Mobile-first and available when they're watching games.

## Audience 4: The Veteran Trader-Bettor (The Hands-On Operator)

> **One-liner:** Decades of cross-market experience. Edwin's profile. The power user.

**Their World:** They've spent decades moving between markets — traditional equities during the day, sports books in the evening, prediction markets when they're available. They have brokerage accounts at Schwab and Webull, accounts on Kalshi, Polymarket, FanDuel, and DraftKings. They've seen every iteration of every market and are first to anything new because they're constantly hunting for an edge.

**Their Pains:** Traditional equity markets shut down before the action they care about starts. Sports betting is a structurally losing proposition (they know the hold percentages). Prediction markets are binary and have terrible liquidity at the moments that matter. Edwin's own experience: $900 disappeared on Polymarket and took 28 hours to get a response, four days to credit — the infrastructure isn't built for serious operators.

**Their Fears:** Wasting time on yet another "innovation" that turns out to be a worse version of what already exists. Missing the early innings of something that genuinely _is_ new.

**Their Secret Hope:** A market that's actually new — not a re-skin of betting, not a re-skin of stocks — but a path-dependent equity asset class that rewards real trading skill and that they can play in the evenings when they're already watching the game anyway.

**Demographic notes:**
- Age 40–55
- Already trades traditional markets during market hours
- Already bets on sports separately
- Understands order books, microstructure, momentum, risk management
- Edwin: _"is the money as important to a person my age? Probably not as someone who's young"_ — motivation is intellectual challenge + leaderboard recognition, not financial necessity

**Acquisition channels:** Press releases (T0, Sport Radar, Rebel partnerships), industry podcasts, professional networks, direct word of mouth.

**Why they switch to InPlay:** A genuinely new asset class with familiar mechanics (order book, continuous pricing). Sophisticated trading infrastructure. Single liquidity pool. Risk-adjusted return leaderboard rewards disciplined approach. Pathway to production trading as a new income stream. The most transparent equity market in the world — _"there's no other company that you could turn on your television and watch firsthand."_

## Cross-Audience Notes

- **The flow is identical, the differences are acquisition.** All four audiences pass through the same onboarding, trading, and referral mechanics. What differs is _how they arrive_ and _which trust signals matter most_.
- **Audience 4 is a stakeholder POV more than a target market centre-of-gravity.** Edwin built InPlay for his own profile, but the marketing dollars and campus programmes are aimed at audiences 1–3. Audience 4's value is product quality (they're the ones who'll catch a broken order book) and credibility (they bring press, advisors, capital).
- **No audience is gated.** Per vision: _"I would shy away from the qualification part... you have to provide fair access."_
