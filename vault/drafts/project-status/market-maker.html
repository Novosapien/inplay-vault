Market maker IN BUILD

The always-on liquidity engine: two-sided quotes on all 170 names, 24 hours a day

Where it stands

The market maker is the most active build in the programme and carries its hardest deadline: quoting live from the NCAA secondary open (26–27 August). The v1.3 Consolidated Build Specification (adopted 24 July) is the single engineering baseline, refined by Edwin's follow-up answers (28 July) and the adopted parts of his later algorithms. The foundations are built and proven; quote construction is the current chapter.

Built and proven

  • Valuation engine — reference price from win probabilities, the $5-per-win model and the off-field term; probability validation at the door.
  • Position and inventory engine — net position, average cost, P&L, inventory lean and the reservation midpoint. 235 automated tests pass; static analysis clean.
  • Event backbone — every input journalled and replayable: a full 2024 NFL game (1,089 probability points) replays from the journal to a byte-identical price stream. This is the audit and debugging backbone for the season.
  • Decimal-exact arithmetic throughout — no floating-point drift in prices or positions.
  • Edwin's spread-width equation (from his ASMM-1 algorithm) assessed and adopted; his inventory guards assessed and rejected with rationale (they would leave books with no bid).

In build now

  • Quote construction — spread width, price ladders, order sizing, seeded randomisation of quantities (prices stay purely algorithmic), cancel-and-replace into tZERO.
  • Then in sequence: the probability poller (live win probabilities ~every 2s per live game), market state (data-freshness bands and liquidity sessions: in-game ~200ms cadence, 30–60s otherwise, earnings windows), and venue sync (reconciling the intended book against tZERO's confirmed book, with the gateway's dead-man heartbeat).
  • Daily reference-feed intake: Edwin's Wednesday drop of expected wins and off-field values for all 170 teams, validated on upload.

Operating agreements (from the July sessions)

  • Quotes rest until fully gone; partial fills are never topped up; on a price move, cancel and repost the remainder at the new price.
  • The market maker is a normal participant posting resting limit orders — users can still match each other directly.
  • Buying power effectively unlimited; the MM buys all unsold IPO shares and warehouses the inventory.
  • Profit is explicitly not a season-1 goal; the behavioural dataset recruits production market makers later.
  • Monitoring dashboard is read-only first (positions and holdings), reusing the standard user inventory APIs; parameter changes stay out of v1.

Open

  • Opening inventory (E27) — the IPO runs on the internal primary plane, so tZERO never sees the allocation; InPlay must publish the day-one book per team.
  • tZERO account permissioning (T1) — the synthetic MM entity in QA, requested via Troy.
  • Quote replenishment discrepancy (E17) — the written spec and Edwin's spoken instruction differ; a correctness ruling is owed.
  • Reference-price layer question (E30) — whether Edwin's later layer is the published price or his simulator; the valuation build holds steady until answered.
  • Compliance check (E33) — two InPlay house accounts interacting on a regulated venue needs the legal read, and tZERO's wash-trade blocking behaviour needs confirming.
  • Remaining parameters: spread floors per state, Edwin's risk constants, venue timeouts and rate limits — tracked in the parameters registry with a status on every number.
