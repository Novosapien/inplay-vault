InPlay Global
Agency Brief: B2C Growth and B2B Media Sales
1. Introduction
InPlay is building the first sports performance exchange: a platform that will allow users to trade
shares in teams based on performance within a defined event season. The model is designed to work
in a way that mirrors traditional share trading, with team prices moving according to demand driven
by on-field performance, off-field developments, and sentiment of teams.
As part of proving the model, creating an early engagement inflection point, and establishing an
ongoing freemium entry route before funded trading is introduced, InPlay will launch a risk-free
Trading Challenge, kicking off on 1 August 2026 to align with the NFL and NCAA Division 1
football seasons. This challenge is intended to be both a major marketing moment and an ongoing
product tool: a freemium participation layer that introduces users to the mechanics of InPlay before
funded trading becomes available soon after.
We are seeking agency partners to help us deliver two connected commercial outcomes against that
launch:
• a B2C growth objective of 750,000 active Trading Challenge users at launch
• a B2B commercial objective of $40–60 million in committed media buying and
sponsorship revenue secured for rollout from 1 August 2026
We are open to appointing separate partners for B2C and B2B, or considering a single agency only
if it can credibly demonstrate best-in-class capability across both.
2. Background and market opportunity
Sport is increasingly moving from passive consumption to active participation. Audiences no longer
only watch games; they follow momentum shifts in real time, react emotionally and intellectually to
key moments, evaluate performance as it changes, and increasingly expect digital products that
allow them to participate in that experience more actively. The market has already evolved in that
direction through gaming, fantasy, betting, prediction markets, second-screen behaviour, and live
social conversation. What remains underdeveloped is a platform that turns that instinctive fan
behaviour into a structured, repeatable, high-engagement participation model built around
performance. InPlay is designed to meet that opportunity.
This is especially relevant now because the value of sport is no longer just in audience size; it is in
attention quality and actionability. Traditional sports media monetises reach and association
extremely well, but much of it still relies on passive formats: stadium branding, broadcast spots,
sponsorship idents, and game-day activations. These environments attract high levels of spend
because they deliver cultural relevance and mass attention, but they offer limited direct
participation. InPlay is built to create a more interactive layer of value: one where users are not
simply exposed to sport, but are continuously engaged in decisions shaped by sport.
The initial focus on the NFL and NCAA Division 1 football is deliberate. Football remains the most
commercially powerful recurring attention system in the United States. It combines scale,
frequency, ritual, rivalry, and national relevance in a way few other properties can. It delivers the

largest recurring audiences in American sport, concentrates attention into premium windows, and
underpins some of the most valuable advertising and sponsorship moments in the market. That
makes it the strongest possible entry point for a product designed around repeated real-time
engagement and premium media value.

The Trading Challenge is the first major proof point of that opportunity. It is a free-to-enter, risk-
free competition designed to replicate the core logic of InPlay in a way that is accessible at scale.

Users participate by making decisions based on changing team performance and sentiment, without
personal financial risk. The challenge is designed to create repeat behaviour throughout the season
through structured competition, tiered participation, leaderboards, rewards, referrals, and sustained
involvement across the biggest football moments in the calendar. The model is intended not only to
drive acquisition, but to create habit, fluency, and long-term participation in the category.
This is also why the B2B opportunity is so significant. InPlay offers media buyers something
different from standard sports inventory. It is not just access to football audiences; it is access to
audiences in a state of repeated participation. That means longer sessions, more frequent return
behaviour, stronger behavioural signals, more integrated sponsor touchpoints, and a platform where
brands can be embedded into action rather than placed around it. InPlay is designed so that
sponsorship can sit within reward systems, category ownership, key interaction points, and repeated
session presence, creating a more measurable and behaviour-linked value proposition for
advertisers.
The commercial rationale for the B2B target follows directly from this. If InPlay achieves 750,000
active users participating at an average of 20 minutes per session, three times per week, across a 12-
week football window, the platform would generate approximately 540 million engagement
minutes before any further upside from broader college football intensity, referral growth, or deeper
repeat behaviour. That level of participation creates a meaningful media and sponsorship product,
especially when paired with exclusivity, category ownership, branded challenges, action-linked
rewards, and season-long partner integration.
InPlay’s sponsorship structure is therefore designed around a base investment and a maximum
ceiling, with advertiser payment tied to delivered engagement minutes rather than flat sponsorship
fees alone. Advertisers are being asked to commit to the upside potential of the highest ceiling, but
actual payment scales according to the minutes delivered in agreed increments. In effect, InPlay
earns in line with delivery, rather than relying on a traditional sponsorship model where payment is
fixed regardless of realised engagement. This is a key reason the B2B target is set at $40–60 million
in committed media buying and sponsorship revenue: it reflects both the scale of the attention
opportunity and the accountability of the commercial model.
In short, InPlay sits at the intersection of three powerful forces: premium football attention, growing
user demand for interactive participation, and advertiser demand for more measurable and
integrated engagement environments. The Trading Challenge is designed to prove that opportunity
at scale.
3. What the Trading Challenge is
The Trading Challenge is a risk-free simulation of the same core logic that will underpin production
trading.
It is designed to:
• allow users to participate in the InPlay experience without personal financial risk

• teach users how the platform works through live participation
• create repeat engagement and competitive behaviour over the course of the season
• act as a freemium entry point into the wider InPlay ecosystem for when production trading
comes online soon after
• give advertisers and sponsors a large-scale, highly engaged audience product from day one
Users will be able to participate across five trading tiers, from rookie through to advanced trader.
Within each tier, rewards will be structured across daily, weekly, monthly, and seasonal timeframes.
The rewards across these five tiers will be cash-based, and reward categories are expected to
include:
• overall money won
• risk-adjusted return
• comeback trader performance
There will also be referral-led reward mechanics and the potential for additional linked incentives,
including simulator cash, merchandise, and advertiser-funded rewards.

7. Referral programme and incentives
InPlay expects to run a two-phase referral programme.
Phase 1: summer referral programme, leading up to 1 August launch
This phase is designed to drive:
• app download
• registration / KYC completion
This direction has come directly from repeated feedback in campus presentations, where students
have expressed confidence that they could drive referral at meaningful scale if a cash-based
incentive were in place.
Our current directional model is:
• the initial 500 students would be incentivised to refer 100 people each
• that would create 49,500 additional referrals
• if that 49,500 audience then goes on to refer 25 each
• the result would be 1,237,500 KYC registrations
The referral rewards in this phase are expected to be primarily cash-based, with tiered incentives for
top performers. This model is not yet fixed, but we want agencies to respond to it seriously,

pressure-test it, and improve it where they believe stronger mechanics exist as part of the wider
goal.
Phase 2: referral programme during the Trading Challenge
This phase is designed to drive:
• challenge participation
• continued engagement during the challenge
• repeat usage strong enough to support the platform’s commercial engagement assumptions
Our current direction is:
• in-season referral incentives may include additional simulator cash, allowing users to
continue participating if they run out of challenge capital

• unused referral-linked simulator cash may potentially convert into vouchers or advertiser-
funded rewards at season end

• lower tiers may include merchandise and other rewards
A further potential prize, raised multiple times during campus presentations, is a top-referrer reward
such as a paid internship opportunity with InPlay. We would like agencies to consider this as
part of the incentive structure, given the level of interest it has already generated.
We are open to agencies improving or refining both phases of this model based on proven
experience and performance evidence.

11. B2B commercial context
InPlay already has an outline sponsorship and inventory structure, but it requires refinement against
final app inventory, ad units, and the strongest commercial sales logic.
The current direction includes:
• four tiers of sponsorship / inventory / placement
• varying levels of exclusivity
• branded content and action-linked integration opportunities
• packaging based around attention, engagement, and repeated behavioural interaction
• the need to avoid cannibalisation across categories, placements, and sponsor rights
The commercial story is built on the idea that users are not merely present but repeatedly active
during peak-emotion games and moments within those games, at a regular cadence throughout the
season. Sponsor value is expected to come from persistent presence across sessions, repeated
interaction points, ownership of key moments, connection to rewards and action, and measurable
engagement rather than static ad delivery alone. The sponsorship structure is intended to align base
commitments and upside with the engagement the platform generates.
Excluded advertiser categories
• gambling
• prediction markets
